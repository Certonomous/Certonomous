# F6b — in-house periodic hill vs ERCOFTAC: results

Date: 2026-08-05. Pre-registration: `F6b_ERCOFTAC_PREREGISTRATION.md`, committed
as `31b0be16` **before the first iteration ran**. Every gate, tolerance,
reference band and prediction quoted below is read from that file, not
composed here. Machine-readable companion: `F6b_runs/gate_result.json`.

## Headline

**Gate V (verification of our own pipeline): PASS, and by two orders of
magnitude more margin than it asked for.** Our own mesh, generated from the
published ERCOFTAC hill polynomial, reattaches at **x/h = 7.6472** against the
benchmark's shipped-mesh value of **7.6439** — a difference of **0.043%**
against a pre-registered tolerance of ±5%. Separation lands at **0.2604**
against the shipped **0.2590**, a difference of 0.0014 h against a tolerance of
±0.05 h.

**Gate P (physics vs ERCOFTAC): FAIL, by over-prediction, exactly as
pre-registered.** Reattachment **7.6472** against the literature band
**[4.21, 4.70]** is **+63% to +82%** depending on which reference in that band
you take, **+72% against the band midpoint 4.455**. The pre-registered
prediction was x_R/h ∈ [7.0, 8.3] and +50% to +95%: **held**. Separation
**0.2604** against the LES band [0.19, 0.20] is +30% to +37% in x/h, inside the
predicted [0.20, 0.30] window: **held**.

**Gate Q (profiles): PASS.** Scaled MAE of |U| at the nine standard stations is
**12.82%** on the medium rung, inside the predicted [10%, 16%].

**And one result nobody registered a prediction for, which is the most
interesting thing here: the finest rung will not converge at all.** See §4.

**The medium rung is a near-replica of the shipped mesh built by a different

along the hill flank where the shipped mesh distributes them by x.

## 2. Geometry verification, done before the pre-registration closed

Our ERCOFTAC polynomial, evaluated at the 121 lower-wall face points of the
benchmark's shipped mesh, agrees with the shipped wall to a **maximum error of
1.30e-7 h** and a mean of 1.56e-8 h. Two independently coded descriptions of
the same hill agreeing to seven decimals. **One of the six published cubic
segments was wrong on the first attempt** — the 30 ≤ x < 40 mm segment's cubic
coefficient — and this check found it immediately, at a maximum wall error of
0.60 h, which is 60% of a hill height. That is what the check is for.

## 3. Gate V — our pipeline against the shipped-mesh answer

| quantity | shipped mesh (2026-07-29) | our coarse | our medium | our fine | Gate V tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| separation x/h | 0.259015 | 0.2789 | **0.2604** | 0.2463 | ±0.05 h → PASS at 0.0014 |
| reattachment x/h | 7.643895 | 7.6436 | **7.6472** | 7.6546 | ±5% → **PASS at 0.043%** |

**Grid sensitivity, Gate V's second clause:** |x_R(fine) − x_R(medium)| /
x_R(medium) = **0.097%**, against a pre-registered threshold of 5%. Across the
whole four-fold cell range from coarse to fine the reattachment point moves
from 7.6436 to 7.6546, a total spread of **1.4%** — well inside the reference
band's own ±5.5% scatter. **Pre-registered prediction 4 said the medium-to-fine
change would be 3–12% and called itself "the weakest claim in this file."** It
was wrong, and wrong in the direction that strengthens the result: the
reattachment point on this case is grid-insensitive to a degree the
pre-registration did not expect.

**Separation is the less converged quantity, and that is worth saying because
the headline number hides it.** x_S/h runs 0.2789 → 0.2604 → 0.2463 across the
ladder, monotone, an 11.7% spread — eight times the reattachment's. It is
moving steadily *toward* the LES band [0.19, 0.20], so the grid, not the model,
carries part of the separation error the shipped-mesh record reported as model
error. Nothing in the 2026-07-29 record could have seen this, because it had
one mesh.

**The fine rung did not reach `residualControl` 1e-6 and is labelled as such,
per the pre-registration's own rule.** At the 6,000-iteration cap its initial
residuals were Ux 4.47e-6 and p 1.67e-5, falling monotonically over four
decades (Ux 8.3e-4 → 2.4e-4 → 1.3e-4 → 3.7e-5 → 1.2e-5 → 4.5e-6 at each
thousand). It is roughly a factor of five short of the declared tolerance, and
its numbers are used above with that stated rather than quietly promoted. The
verdict does not lean on it: Gate V passes on the medium rung, which converged
in 5,997 iterations with the solver's own `SIMPLE solution converged` sentence
in the log.

**A note on that sentence, because the 2026-07-29 record could not print it.**
That run inherited `residualControl { p 1e-15; }` from the shipped case, a
tolerance no GAMG pressure solve reaches on this mesh, so simpleFoam could
never declare convergence and the lab's gate checker returned a documented
false negative. Setting a reachable 1e-6 was one of the three declared
deviations in this pre-registration, and two of the four rungs here print the
sentence. That defect is now closed by construction rather than by argument.

## 4. The finest rung does not converge, and this is the finding

The veryfine rung (62,400 cells) ran the full 6,000-iteration cap and **does
not converge; its residuals never fall at all**. Sampled every thousand
iterations, the initial residual on Ux reads 1.27e-2, 1.07e-2, 6.5e-3, 1.06e-2,
7.8e-3, 8.8e-3 and on pressure 0.259, 0.218, 0.220, 0.227, 0.265, 0.234, at
iterations 1,000 through 6,000. That is not slow convergence. It is a limit
cycle six thousand iterations wide, at a residual level three to four decades
above where the three coarser rungs stood at the same iteration count, and
`SIMPLE solution converged` appears zero times.

**Its wall field is visibly not a steady separation bubble, and that is the
cleanest statement of the failure.** The three converged rungs each produce
exactly two skin-friction sign changes on the lower wall — one separation, one
reattachment. This rung produces **twenty-two**, scattered from x/h = 0.047 to
8.96, and its station-profile MAE against the LES field doubles to 25.8% from
the 12.5–12.8% the other three agree on. No separation or reattachment number
is quoted from this rung anywhere in this record, because it does not have one.

**This was not predicted and no gate covers it.** Recording it as a result
rather than as a failed run, with the honest statement of what is and is not
known:

- **What is measured:** three rungs converge or nearly converge on the same
  answer to 1.4%, and the fourth, refined by the same recipe and passing the
  same mesh gates, does not converge at all.
- **The most likely reading, and it is a reading:** at this near-wall and
  shear-layer resolution the periodic hill's separated shear layer stops
  admitting a steady solution and the steady SIMPLE algorithm limit-cycles
  around an unsteady flow. The periodic hill at Re_H = 10595 is a genuinely
  unsteady flow whose reference data are time-averaged LES; a steady RANS
  solution of it exists only as long as the discretization is diffusive enough
  to suppress the unsteadiness. That reading has a cheap discriminator: run the
  same mesh with `pimpleFoam` and see whether the residual plateau resolves
  into a periodic signal with a Strouhal number.
- **A competing reading that has NOT been excluded:** the relaxation factors
  (0.5/0.5/0.7/0.7) are the shipped case's, tuned for the shipped resolution,
  and a 62,400-cell mesh may simply need tighter under-relaxation. This is
  cheaper still to test and should be tested first. **Until it is, the unsteady
  explanation is a hypothesis, not a finding, and it is written here as one.**
- **What it does not do:** it does not disturb Gate V or Gate P. Both are
  decided on the medium rung, whose answer is corroborated by the coarse rung
  below it, by the fine rung above it, and by an independently meshed
  independently solved shipped case, all four agreeing within 1.4%.

## 5. Gate P — the physics, against ERCOFTAC

| quantity | our medium rung | reference | source | deviation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reattachment x/h | **7.6472** | 4.6–4.7 | Fröhlich, Mellen, Rodi, Temmerman & Leschziner (2005), *JFM* **526**, 19–66, LES, as hosted by NASA TMR ("reattachment is near 4.6-4.7h") | **+63% to +66%** |
| reattachment x/h | 7.6472 | 4.69 | Breuer, Peller, Rapp & Manhart (2009), *Computers & Fluids* **38**, 433–457, as tabulated on ERCOFTAC KBwiki UFR 3-30 Evaluation | **+63%** |
| reattachment x/h | 7.6472 | 4.21 | Rapp & Manhart (2011), *Exp. Fluids* **51**, 247–269, PIV/LDA water channel | **+82%** |
| reattachment x/h | 7.6472 | 4.455 (band midpoint) | the three above | **+72%** |
| separation x/h | 0.2604 | 0.19–0.20 | Fröhlich et al. 2005; Breuer et al. 2009 (the experiment could not measure it) | +30% to +37% in x/h, i.e. +0.06 h |
| profile scaled MAE of \|U\|, nine stations | 12.82% | — | vs the Breuer LES field shipped on the identical geometry | — |

**Verdict: Gate P FAILS, and the failure is the point.** Stock kOmegaSST
over-predicts the periodic-hill recirculation length by roughly 72% against the
midpoint of the literature's own band, on a mesh this lab built, verified
against the published geometry to seven decimals, and demonstrated to be
grid-insensitive on this quantity to 1.4% over a four-fold cell range. That is
a much stronger statement than the 2026-07-29 record could make, which had one
mesh and could not separate model error from grid error at all.

**This does not go on the wall and is not a capability.** It goes in
`NOT_PASSING_REGISTER.md` alongside the 2026-07-29 entry, and it is what a
data-driven closure correction on this case has to close.

## 7. Cost

| item | core-min |
| --- | --- |
| feasibility (coarse, 200 iterations) | 0.10 |
| coarse gate rung | 1.71 |
| medium gate rung | 6.79 |
| fine gate rung | 16.06 |
| veryfine rung (non-converging, §4) | 25.57 |
| meshing, checkMesh, analysis | under 1 |
| **total** | **≈ 50.2** |

Against an 80 core-min budget. Roughly half of that total bought the rung that
did not converge, which is the honest accounting: the anomaly in §4 is the most
expensive single line here and it is also the one this record would be weakest
without. Every solve ran serially on one core rather than
on the 2-core cap: at this case size the parallel overhead is real and, more
importantly, serial sampling removes the processor-boundary line-sampling
artifact the 2026-07-29 record had to document (12.51% serial against 12.95% on
4 ranks for the identical field). Four rungs on four cores is inside the same
resource envelope and the profile numbers here need no such caveat.

**A bug found and fixed in the analysis, of a class the record has met
before.** The first run of `gate.py` returned a profile MAE of 1683%. The
station files this case writes are named `line_k_nut_omega_p_U.xy` and the
reader assumed `line_U.xy`'s column layout. This is the same trap that left
`gate_result.json`'s profile metric literally `null` in the 2026-07-29 record —
that script looked for a file named `line_U.xy` and silently found nothing.
`gate.py` now parses the field order out of the filename rather than assuming
it, and says so in its own docstring.
