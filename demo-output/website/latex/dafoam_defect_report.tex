\begin{abstract}
\noindent
On a 2{,}777-cell OpenFOAM case run under \artifact{dafoam/opt-packages:latest}
(DAFoam 5.0.0, OpenFOAM v2506), varying \emph{only} the mesh decomposition moves the
analytic \(\mathrm{d}C_D/\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{shape})\) gradient from
\(0.00054\%\) agreement with its own finite difference (\texttt{simple} 4x1x1) to
\(8.95\%\) disagreement (\texttt{scotch}, the shipped default), with the converged primal
invariant to five significant figures and the adjoint right-hand side invariant to
\(4.8\times10^{-6}\). The linear solve is not at fault: it converges cleanly
(\texttt{PetscConvergedReason: 2}, true residual \(1.7\times10^{-7}\) in the
unpreconditioned norm) and is unmoved by a tolerance tightened four decades. The
discriminating measurement is a cross-residual: the converged \texttt{scotch} adjoint
vector, permuted to serial ordering by integer \texttt{decomposePar} addressing and
evaluated under the serial reverse-AD operator, leaves a residual of \(329\times\lVert
b\rVert\) against that operator's own \(1.14\times10^{-4}\) floor --- concentrated in the
\(x\)-momentum rows of partition-interface cells. The parallel matrix-free
transposed-Jacobian product is not the transpose Jacobian of the residual it claims to
differentiate.

What carries it is now named to one word. Changing \texttt{div(phi,U)} from
\texttt{bounded Gauss linearUpwind limited} to \texttt{bounded Gauss linearUpwind default}
--- same scheme family, same order, the same halo-exchanged gradient-correction term still
evaluated, with only the limiter's min/max selection over the stencil removed --- takes the
gradient error from \(8.95\%\) to \(0.849\%\), the adjoint Krylov count from 719 to 41, and
the cross-residual from \(329\times\lVert b\rVert\) to
\(1.35\times10^{-2}\times\lVert b\rVert\): a 24{,}270-fold collapse of the operator error,
recomputed from the raw dumped vectors by an independent sweep. The other lever that cleans
this gradient does not clean the operator. Swapping the farfield \texttt{U} boundary
condition leaves the transposed-Jacobian product wrong at \(1.047\times\lVert b\rVert\) ---
163{,}548 times its own serial floor --- underneath a \texttt{check\_totals} reading
\(0.019\%\). An earlier revision of this document called that configuration clean. It is not
safe; it is quiet.

The same recorded branch then broke a case with no processor boundary in it at all.
Installed on a 4{,}032-cell structured NACA0012, the limiter drives the \emph{serial}
adjoint to \(92.8\%\) against its own step-stable finite difference, with one of eight
gradient components sign-flipped --- and the identical one-word edit returns \(0.121\%\).
Both decompositions of that case agree to \(3.9\times10^{-4}\) while both are \(92.8\%\)
wrong: invariance certifies consistency, not correctness.

We report the phenomenon, the discriminators that convicted the operator and exonerated
every other candidate, the localization, the campaign that named the branch and separated
the fix from the disguise, the serial variant and its single-process reproducer, why no
published verification could have caught any of it, and two adjacent findings from the same
forensic arc: an IDWarp degenerate-branch guard that differentiates to a hard zero at
exactly the state every gradient evaluation uses (root-caused, patched in four lines, and
answering a five-year-old open upstream issue), and an ASM sub-block ILU factorization whose
failure is broken by the PETSc developers' own sanctioned escalation to complete LU.

A fourth section, added 2026-08-08 and extended 2026-08-10, reports a failure of a different kind, found while
carrying that last remedy to a transonic case --- and corrects a claim of our own. This lab's
record held that the ONERA M6 discrete adjoint was blocked by conditioning at every mesh size
tested, \texttt{PetscConvergedReason: -5} from 21{,}840 to 99{,}840 cells. Every one of those
runs set \texttt{transonicPCOption: 2}, which is dead code for the solver that read it:
\artifact{DAResidualRhoSimpleCFoam.C:173} implements only \texttt{== 1}, and \texttt{== 2}
lives in a different solver's source. The option parses, warns nothing, and takes no branch.
Setting it to \texttt{1} --- one token, on the identical configuration, same mesh, same
partition, same coloring cache, stock ILU --- converts both diverged solves into
\texttt{reason 2} at 368 and 383 iterations, and the converged \(C_L\) solve begins at
\(1.839195903440\times10^{-1}\), the number the record's own run stalled on, and descends
through it. A pre-registered negative control at the record value reproduces the recorded
failure bit for bit --- iteration-0 residual, the whole printed stall sequence, the terminal
denormal \(3.945602898014\times10^{-308}\) --- and a pre-registered finite-difference table
verifies two of four probed gradient components at 0.18\% and 0.93\%, with the other two
reported unverdicted rather than forced. The next rung up, 42{,}120 cells, converges too --- 987
and 1171 iterations, with all three of its probed components evaluable, step-consistent and
inside 0.28\% --- so the correction rests on two rungs rather than one. The third rung,
79{,}560 cells, does \emph{not} converge: it stagnates flat at a cap 1.6 times the predicted
requirement with memory comfortable, which places a ceiling on the reopened ladder somewhere
between 42{,}120 and 79{,}560 cells and breaks the two-rung iteration-scaling law by kind
rather than by degree. The wall was real; at the rungs below that ceiling its cause was
misattributed. A second silent configuration is reported alongside: the
wrapper writes the primal end state back into time~0, so the cold start you assumed is not the
start that ran. And a third, with the widest audience of the three: the adjoint solver calls
\texttt{KSPSetFromOptions} and then overrides it at twelve later call sites, so
\texttt{-ksp\_type}, \texttt{-pc\_type} and \texttt{-sub\_pc\_type} are accepted without
error and discarded --- while \texttt{-ksp\_monitor\_true\_residual} and
\texttt{-ksp\_view\_pmat} work perfectly. The same channel that hands a user a correct
diagnosis silently drops the remedy it implies, which this lab established from both ends:
ten candidate causes of the ceiling are eliminated by measurement through that channel ---
including diagonal spread, by the decisive observation that 14.40 decades converges and 14.47
does not, and non-normality, by the stalling rung measuring \(1.62\times\) \emph{less}
non-normal than the converging one --- and every remedy those eliminations point to next is one
the channel discards.
\textbf{Nothing in this document has been filed upstream.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary of findings}
%======================================================================

Seven findings, from four weeks of forensic work on the MACH-Aero/DAFoam toolchain. Three
of them are one defect class --- a \emph{branch} recorded on a differentiated path, whose
selection the reverse sweep gets wrong --- in two repositories, in parallel and in serial.
The fourth is a wall in a shipped default with a remedy that is one option away. The last three
share a shape with each other and not with the first four: configuration the tool accepts and
does not run --- an option that is dead code for the solver that read it, a state rewrite that
no log line announces, and an entire PETSc option channel that answers diagnostics faithfully
and discards the remedies typed into it.

\paragraph{What links the first three.} A guard, a limiter, and a switching boundary
condition are the same kind of object: a \texttt{min}, a \texttt{max}, an \texttt{abs}, or an
\texttt{if} evaluated on data and then recorded on a tape. Reverse-mode AD differentiates
the branch that was taken, correctly and by design. What it cannot do is know that the
branch it recorded is the wrong function of the inputs --- a constant where the true map has
a first-order term (D2), or a selection whose stencil reaches across a processor face and
whose reverse does not (D1), or one that is simply mis-swept in a single process (D1s). The
class is cheap to hunt once it is named: every member here is removable by a one-line
dictionary edit or a four-line derivative, which makes it the cheapest discriminator
available, and it was one such edit that closed D1's mechanism hunt.

\paragraph{What links all seven.} They are invisible to the checks that ship with the tools.
D2's discarded term sums to \emph{exactly} zero over the surface, and the sum is the only
quantity upstream's test asserts on. D1 produces a gradient that is self-consistent under the
decomposition it was measured on, and no published verification varies the decomposition.
D1s is self-consistent across decompositions too --- it passes the invariance gate this lab
adopted after D1, to \(3.9\times10^{-4}\), while being 92.8\% wrong. D3 announces itself
loudly, but the documented remedy ladder does not contain the remedy. D4, D5 and D6 are the
degenerate case of the same problem: the instrument reports the dictionary, and the dictionary
is not the run --- an option echoed back verbatim while no branch takes it, a start state
replaced between runs with nothing printed, and a command line that is parsed, delivered, and
overwritten before it reaches the solve. D6 is the sharpest of the three, because the same
channel that drops the user's remedy hands back their diagnosis correctly. Each was found by measuring the thing the
instrument was not looking at.

\paragraph{What D4 and D5 cost, and why they are here.} Neither is a wrong number. D4 produced
nine days of correct measurements assembled into one incorrect sentence and one over-general
one --- the expensive kind of defect, because every individual figure survives the audit. The
first was causal: a wall attributed to conditioning, and it dissolves when the option is
activated. The second outlived it by weeks and is subtler: the recorded finding that
strengthening the preconditioner reintroduces collapse on this family, which made every
documented escalation look counterproductive. That observation turns out to be \emph{true and
mesh-dependent} --- it reproduces on a live preconditioner one rung up --- so what the dead
lever cost there was not the finding but its width (Section~\ref{sec:artifact}). A wrong wall
stops work; a correct observation recorded at the wrong scope redirects it. D5
produced no wrong conclusion at all, on this evidence, and is reported because the audit that
established that is the only reason anyone knows. A defect report that only lists other
people's wrong numbers is not the same document as one that lists the ways a toolchain can
quietly make you wrong.

\subsection{The phenomenon}

The case is A4, a coarse Ahmed body: 2{,}777 cells, snappyHexMesh-class grid with
refinement interfaces, \artifact{DASimpleFoam}, kOmegaSST, one FFD shape design variable.
The instrument is OpenMDAO's \artifact{check\_totals}, whose finite-difference column is
computed \emph{per run, inside that run's own} \artifact{check\_totals} --- not computed
once and reused. Only the decomposition varies across the rows of
Table~\ref{tab:phenomenon}; verified by \texttt{diff} of every run directory against the
source case (the np runs carry a byte-identical \artifact{runScript.py}; the \texttt{simple}
runs differ by exactly one inserted \artifact{decomposeParDict} daOption line; the tolerance
run by exactly the \artifact{gmresRelTol} value), and by reading the
\texttt{Decomposition method} line back out of each log.

Four properties of this table decide everything that follows.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The primal does not move.} Converged baseline \(C_D\) spans
0.15296979--0.15297237 across \emph{all} configurations --- invariant to five significant
figures. np=1 and np=4 \texttt{scotch} differ by \(5.5\times10^{-7}\) absolute,
\(3.6\times10^{-6}\) relative: separately reconverged SIMPLE solves at
\artifact{primalMinResTol} 1e-4, i.e.\ reconvergence noise, four orders of magnitude below
the 8.95\% gradient shift. The \texttt{simple} arm --- the arm with the \emph{correct}
gradient --- differs from np=1 by \emph{more} than \texttt{scotch} does.
\item \textbf{The finite difference does not move meaningfully.} The FD column spans
(2.4178--2.4265)e-01, a 0.36\% spread, against an 8.95\% analytic excursion.
\item \textbf{Rank count is not the trigger.} np=2 \texttt{scotch} sits at the np=1 floor
(0.26\%); np=3 is catastrophic in one direction (6.05\%, analytic high) and np=4 in the
other (8.95\%, analytic low); np=4 \texttt{simple} 4x1x1 is the cleanest configuration in
the table. What changes between the dirty and clean rows is the \emph{shape and orientation
of the cut}.
\item \textbf{The solve is not the problem.} Tightening \artifact{gmresRelTol} from 1e-6 to
1e-10 costs 811 GMRES iterations instead of 719 and changes the answer by nothing. The
np=4 \texttt{scotch} solve reports \texttt{PetscConvergedReason: 2} at final true residual
\(1.749572\times10^{-7}\), and DAFoam's own solver configuration makes that literal:
\artifact{DALinearEqn.C} sets \texttt{KSPSetPCSide(PC\_RIGHT)} and
\texttt{KSPSetNormType(KSP\_NORM\_UNPRECONDITIONED)}, so ``converged in the unpreconditioned
norm'' means what it says.
\end{enumerate}

This is the signature of a \emph{converged wrong answer}. It is also the signature that
makes the defect survivable in production: nothing in the solver's own telemetry is
anomalous.

\subsection{The discriminators}

Four candidate carriers were named in advance and executed cheapest-first
(\artifact{DISCRIMINATORS\_A4\_decomposition\_mechanism.md}; run artifacts under
\artifact{/home/ubuntu/certonomous-runs/W4-a4-discriminators/}, with per-arm directories,
\artifact{ledger.txt}, \artifact{runScript\_w4.py}, \artifact{build\_maps.py},
\artifact{analyze\_mats.py}).

\paragraph{M3(a) --- the primal, exonerated at zero cost.} Point 1 above, plus this

\subsection{The objection this measurement must survive}

A knowledgeable reader will ask: is the \texttt{scotch} operator perhaps the \emph{exact}
adjoint of a different-but-equivalent parallel discretization? If decomposition changed the
discrete residual itself at processor boundaries, the \texttt{scotch} adjoint could be
consistent with \emph{its} residual while differing from serial, and its
\(1.2\times10^{-3}\) self-consistency would even fit that story.

It does not survive the finite-difference column. The FD in Table~\ref{tab:phenomenon} is
computed per run, inside each arm's own \artifact{check\_totals}. An adjoint that were the
exact adjoint of an equivalent parallel discretization would match the finite difference of
\emph{that} discretization. The \texttt{scotch} analytic 2.2086e-01 disagrees by 8.95\% with
its own decomposition's FD of 2.4258e-01. Combined with the fact that OpenFOAM processor
patches evaluate the same interior face fluxes --- no legitimate \(O(1)\) residual-function
difference is available --- and that \(b\) maps invariantly at \(4.8\times10^{-6}\), the
supported conclusion is the strong one: \textbf{the parallel operator is not the transpose
Jacobian of the residual it claims to differentiate.}

This gap was not acknowledged in the original mechanism record; the supervisor sweep found
it and closed it from the prior record (\artifact{VERIFICATION\_A4\_mechanism\_supervisor\_sweep.md},
\commit{8e0a08bc}, Axis 4). It is stated here in the body, not in a footnote, because it is
the first objection a maintainer will raise.

\subsection{Sign-convention provenance, stated against ourselves}
\label{sec:signconv}

The values in Table~\ref{tab:crossres} appear in no log, and the original record's caption
cited a log that contradicts them on its face. The instrument
(\artifact{runScript\_w4.py}, tasks \artifact{w4\_dump} / \artifact{w4\_crossres}) computed
\texttt{res = Atpsi - b}, whereas the system OpenMDAO seeds is \(A^{\mathsf T}\psi = -b\).
\emph{(Past tense as of 2026-08-07: the instrument has since been repaired ---
see the note closing this subsection. Every number in this document was measured under the
convention described here.)}
Every arm's log therefore prints the sign-degenerate \texttt{ratio=2.000000e+00} for its
own-operator check, and none of the record's own-operator numbers --- the
\(1.14\times10^{-4}\) floor, the \(1.2\times10^{-3}\) self-consistency, the
\(2.083\times10^{-5}\) --- appears in any log.

The tabulated values are exact offline sign corrections, \(\mathrm{res} + 2b\), recomputed
from the dumped vectors (\artifact{w4x\_res\_*.npy}, \artifact{w4\_Atpsi}, \artifact{w4\_b}).
The correction is exact, and the adversarial sweep reproduces every digit of it three
independent ways:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Recomputation from the dumps} with the sweep's own scripts: np=1
\(1.140697\times10^{-4}\), \texttt{scotch} \(3.288078\times10^{2}\) (absolute
\(6.004094\times10^{1}\), max entry 42.25), \texttt{simple} \(9.367397\times10^{-2}\).
\item \textbf{An independently rebuilt permutation}, constructed from
\artifact{cellProcAddressing} / \artifact{faceProcAddressing} and the
\artifact{AdjointIndexing} dumps by the sweep's own parsers, bit-for-bit identical to the
session's map in \texttt{tgt} and \texttt{sgn} --- and validated on a channel the original
session never used: the AdjointIndexing dumps carry cell and face centre coordinates, and
mapped np=4 coordinates match np=1 coordinates at \textbf{max \(\lvert\Delta xyz\rvert =
0.0\)} over all 26{,}149 (\texttt{scotch}) and 26{,}227 (\texttt{simple}) entries. Every entry
of the map is thereby validated exactly, independent of any solved field.
\item \textbf{A fresh serial container run}: a new case directory staged from the same
source, fresh serial primal, \artifact{w4\_crossres} applied to \(\psi\) vectors mapped by
the sweep's own permutation. Raw \texttt{scotch} ratio \(3.288139\times10^{2}\);
sign-corrected \(3.288078\times10^{2}\); np=1 control \(1.140697\times10^{-4}\);
\texttt{simple} \(9.367397\times10^{-2}\)
(\artifact{W4-a4-verify-audit/audit\_crossres.log}, 9~s, 0.30 core-min).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Localization}

The 60.04 cross-residual is not spread over the domain. By state group, \textbf{59.78 of the
60.04 norm sits in \(U_0\) (\(x\)-momentum) rows}. By cell, entries with \(\lvert r\rvert >
0.5\) number 15, of which \textbf{13 sit on \texttt{scotch} partition-interface cells and 2 at
graph distance one} --- none farther. All 15 sit on \artifact{cellLevel} 0 cells, away from the
mesh's refinement interfaces. The two worst (42.25 and 42.24) sit at \(y = -0.1947\) and
\(y = +0.1949\): the \(y\)-normal cut signature.

Three structural facts sharpen this.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It is not a many-rank corner effect.} The worst cells each touch exactly
\emph{one} foreign rank through exactly \emph{one} processor face; cells touching two to four
foreign faces carry \emph{smaller} residuals.
\item \textbf{Cut orientation, not cut jaggedness alone, sets the magnitude.} The hierarchy
in Table~\ref{tab:phenomenon} is orientation-ordered: \(x\)-normal planar slabs 0.00054\%,
\(y\)-normal planar slabs 0.47\%, \texttt{scotch}'s jagged mixed-orientation boundary 8.95\%
--- and the worst cross-residual entries sit on \(y\)-normal processor faces.
\item \textbf{The same instrument on the clean arm is five thousand times quieter.} Applied
to the \texttt{simple} 4x1x1 \(\psi\), the serial operator maxes at \textbf{0.008439} on that
arm's own interface cells (9 of its top 10 entries sit on them) --- \(5{,}006\times\) below
\texttt{scotch}'s 42.25. Had \texttt{simple}'s residual also been huge, the test would have
proven nothing.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The hanging-node hypothesis, refuted backwards.} The natural first guess on a
refinement-interface mesh is that partition cuts crossing hanging nodes are the trigger. The
supervisor sweep re-classified all 7{,}843 internal faces itself, from
\artifact{owner}/\artifact{neighbour}/\artifact{cellLevel} and the archived
\artifact{cellDecomposition} maps: 456 refinement-interface faces, of which \texttt{scotch}
cuts \textbf{4} (0.88\%) and \texttt{simple} 4x1x1 cuts \textbf{68} (14.91\%).
\emph{The decomposition that cuts seventeen times more refinement interfaces is the accurate
one.} The hypothesis is refuted, not merely unconfirmed.

\subsection{The mechanism, named at operator level}
\label{sec:mechop}

\paragraph{Named.} Under decomposition, DAFoam v5's reverse-mode AD product
\((\partial R/\partial W)^{\mathsf T} v\) --- the global-tape shell operator the adjoint KSP
iterates (\artifact{initializeGlobalADTape4dRdWT} / \artifact{dRdWTMatVecMultFunction},
\artifact{DASolver.C:1364ff}) and the \artifact{calcJacTVecProduct} API that reverses the same
recorded computation --- differs from the true transposed Jacobian of the discrete residual
at partition-interface cells, by amounts that depend on the cut's shape and orientation.

The two parallel AD paths sit \emph{together} on the wrong side: they agree with each other
to \(1.2\times10^{-3}\) of \(\lVert b\rVert\) while both sit \(3.3\times10^{2}\) of
\(\lVert b\rVert\) from the serially evaluated truth. The defect is therefore common to the
recorded reverse computation, not to one call site. GMRES then solves the wrong system
exactly; the adjoint inherits \(O(1)\) error along the directions the wrong operator
mislabels (hence the \(16.5\times\) norm inflation); and the gradient contraction
\(\psi^{\mathsf T}\,\partial R/\partial X_v\) delivers the 8.95\%. Every observed symptom
follows: converged wrong answer, tolerance invariance, decomposition dependence,
primal/FD/RHS invariance, and the near-invariance of the lab's conformal-mesh cases.

\paragraph{Not claimed, at the time this was first written.} The defective source line. The
candidate surface was stated as the reverse-AD handling of inter-processor halo exchange
inside the recorded residual evaluation --- the tape over OpenFOAM processor-boundary
updates --- and pinning the line requires an instrumented rebuild of
\artifact{libDASolver.so}. What was pinned: the subsystem, the spatial signature
(interface-cell \(x\)-momentum rows), the orientation dependence, and the discriminating
instrument that any candidate fix must satisfy.

\paragraph{What has since been pinned.} The construct the tape records. A campaign
registered to attack a different weakness came back with the carrier in the case's own
\artifact{fvSchemes}: the slope limiter's \texttt{min}/\texttt{max} selection over a stencil
that reaches halo cells. One word removes it, and removing it collapses the operator error
by four and a half orders of magnitude (Section~\ref{sec:branch}). The line is still not
pinned --- but the search space has narrowed from ``the tape over halo exchange'' to ``the
reverse sweep of \texttt{cellLimited}/\texttt{limitedGrad}'', and it now has a
\emph{single-process} reproducer to instrument (Section~\ref{sec:serial}), which is far
cheaper than a parallel one.

\paragraph{A continuum, not a binary.} The FD-exact \texttt{simple} 4x1x1 \(\psi\) still
leaves \(9.4\times10^{-2}\) of \(\lVert b\rVert\) under the serial operator --- roughly
\(800\times\) the np=1 floor --- while its gradient is correct to 0.00054\%. Every np=4
operator tested differs measurably from serial; \texttt{scotch}'s jagged cut is the
catastrophic point on a continuum, not a separate species.

\subsection{A usability finding recorded on the way}

The last and most expensive discriminator --- run with coloring disabled --- could not
deliver its number, and the reason is worth reporting to upstream in its own right.

\texttt{adjUseColoring: False} \textbf{crashes the v5 mphys Krylov path by construction}:
\artifact{mphys\_dafoam.solve\_linear} skips \artifact{runColoring()} when the option is
False, but \artifact{calcdRdWT} unconditionally calls \artifact{readJacConColoring()}, which

\artifact{runColoring()} unless \artifact{adjUseColoring} is true;
\artifact{DASolver.C:1043} calls \artifact{readJacConColoring()} unconditionally; and
\artifact{DAColoring::validateColoring} (beginning line 931) reaches its

The sweep's recommendation is adopted here as protocol, because it is strictly stronger than a
second step size and costs nothing: for this symptom class, reconstruct the one-sided slopes
from the FD legs' printed objective values. It belongs in the registration, not in the
post-mortem.

\subsection{What the serial variant is, and what it changes}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It is decomposition-independent, and it broke a family property before anyone
had written that property down.} Every defect this lab had traced in this toolchain vanished
at np=1. This one does not.
\item \textbf{It is the same object as D1.} \texttt{cellLimited}'s \texttt{min}/\texttt{max}
stencil selection, recorded on the reverse tape, wrong through a different excitation: a
parallel inconsistency on the Ahmed family, a serial contraction error on the airfoil. On A4
the identical token pair reads only 0.34\% at np=1 --- the serial excitation is
\emph{case-dependent}, which is exactly what a wrongly-differentiated \emph{data-dependent}
branch would produce, since different meshes activate different branches at the converged
state.
\item \textbf{It is far cheaper to file.} A maintainer needs one process, one 4{,}032-cell
structured case, and one word of \artifact{fvSchemes}. No MPI in the loop, no partitioner, no
mapping instrument, no cross-residual protocol. The instrumented-rebuild search space is
unchanged --- the \texttt{cellLimited}/\texttt{limitedGrad} reverse sweep --- and now has a
single-process reproducer to instrument.
\item \textbf{It costs the campaign's framing a clause.} ``The branch defect is a parallel
inconsistency'' is now measured to have an exception: the branch can be wrong serially, with
the parallel machinery innocent.
\item \textbf{It cost this lab an instrument's warrant.} The decomposition-invariance gate
adopted after A4 passes here to \(3.9\times10^{-4}\) on a case that is 92.8\% wrong.
\emph{Invariance instruments detect inconsistency between two evaluations of the same tape; a
tape that is wrong identically in both sails through every one of them.} Never promote an
invariance pass to a correctness verdict unless at least one side is anchored to an external
reference --- an own-run finite difference with a step check, or an independently evaluated
operator.
\end{itemize}

Three facts fall straight out of Table~\ref{tab:papers}.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{No paper ever varies the decomposition of anything.} Decomposition is not an

counts. The two papers that verify DAFoam's v1 operator in (implied) parallel never state

\item \textbf{The operator our defect lives in has exactly one published accuracy

CPU core ``allows us to isolate the impact of parallel communication on the performance'' ---
and confirms the gap in its conclusions: \emph{``We do not have scalability data for the
Jacobian-free (operator overloading) and the full-code AD (operator overloading) options
because we run the adjoint computation only in serial in the ADODG Case 3 (Sec. 5.1).''}
\item \textbf{No verification anywhere combines a refinement-interface mesh with a stated
graph partitioning.} The one snappy-mesh verification --- C\&F 2018's Ahmed body, our
defect's own geometry family --- is of the v1 explicitly FD-assembled Jacobian, an
architecture in which a tape-recording defect cannot exist, at an unstated decomposition.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The corpus checks the derivative class this defect spares.} The serial variant of
Section~\ref{sec:serial} looks at first like it should embarrass point 2 rather than support
it: if the limiter's tape can be 92.8\% wrong in a single process, why did the corpus's one
\emph{serial} verification of the shipped operator family return ten-digit agreement? Two
answers, both readable straight off Table~\ref{tab:papers}, and together they are the sharpest
statement of the gap this section can make.

\emph{First, the corpus's parallel evidence is entirely about the other architecture.} The
only two verifications run in (implied) parallel --- and the only one run on a
refinement-interface mesh, C\&F 2018's Ahmed body, our defect's own geometry family --- measure
the v1 explicitly FD-assembled Jacobian. A finite-difference column re-evaluates the residual,
branches and all: branch \emph{selection} is differenced there, not recorded. A mis-taped
branch cannot exist in that architecture. Every parallel accuracy number in the corpus is a
measurement of the derivative class this defect spares, taken in the configuration where it
could not appear.

\emph{Second, the one measurement that does exercise the recorded tape is a single case at a
single converged state, and our own arms show why that cannot generalise for a data-dependent
branch.} The same \texttt{cellLimited}/\texttt{linearUpwind} token pair reads 92.8\% at np=1 on
the structured NACA0012 and 0.34\% at np=1 on the Ahmed body. A branch that is not activated
asymmetrically at the state you verify at is a branch you verify past. Ten digits on one
structured wing certify that wing's tape at that state --- which is precisely what the survey
claims and no more.

\paragraph{A published claim, corrected against ourselves.} An earlier draft of our own
upstream report asserted that ``the toolchain's journal paper reports average adjoint
derivative error under 0.1\% at up to 1536 cores'' and that our measurement therefore
contradicts a peer-reviewed number. Reading the paper in full showed that sentence to be
\emph{wrong twice}. The abstract joins two disjoint experiments with an ``and'': the 1536
cores is a runtime-only table on a 10.1M-cell structured mesh; the \(<\)0.1\% is a separate
\(\sim\)103k-cell accuracy study at an unstated processor count. \textbf{No accuracy number at
any stated core count exists in that paper.} And both \(<\)0.1\% figures attach to the v1
explicit-FD-Jacobian architecture --- the same paper pairs them with ``Implementing the
Jacobian-free approach in DAFoam \ldots\ will be conducted in the future work.'' The 0.1\%
scale is the FD-partials error floor, not a parallel-consistency scale.

This \emph{strengthens} the case rather than weakening it: the report does not contradict a
published measurement, it fills a hole the survey's own conclusions declare.

\paragraph{No archival method paper covers the shipped architecture.} The DAFoam
publications page lists no framework paper after 2020; the \artifact{mdolab/dafoam} README's
citation section names exactly the two He et al.\ papers, tied to no version; a JOSS search
finds nothing; Zenodo records 15636000 (v4.0.2) and 20045081 (v5.0.0) are software deposits,
not papers. The v2.2 release note that shipped the Jacobian-free adjoint cites no paper for
it either. \emph{The v4/v5 rewrite we measured has no archival method paper at all}; the
citation trail for the shipped matrix-free adjoint ends at papers describing other
architectures.

\paragraph{Was the defect class disclosed anywhere?} Two quotes were fetched for exactly this
question, and neither is a tape caveat. C\&F 2018 \S2.9 names interprocessor boundary-patch
state updates as \emph{``essential for accurately computing the adjoint derivative''} --- for
the v1 FD path, where they are handled by executing
\artifact{U.correctBoundaryConditions()} per perturbation. The v5 tape must \emph{record}
that same coupling, and its interface-cell rows are exactly where our cross-residual
concentrates. PAS 2019 \S A.4 acknowledges that \emph{``some code structures remain a
challenge to differentiate, including pointers and MPI calls''}, and that ADflow's parallel
derivative correctness rests on manual assembly using properties ``that the AD code cannot,
in general, safely assume'' --- a statement about AD-across-MPI as a hazard class, made about
ADflow's development choices.

No paper states a halo-exchange tape caveat or a partition-consistency assumption for
DAFoam's operator-overloading path. There is no sentence a maintainer could point to as prior
disclosure. What the quotes support is gentler and more useful: \textbf{the defect sits in a
subsystem whose verification the papers explicitly scoped out, in a region their own earlier
work flagged as accuracy-critical.}

\section{The IDWarp rotation guard}
\label{sec:idwarp}
%======================================================================

This defect is independent of Section~\ref{sec:decomp} in code and in provenance --- it is
geometric, serial, and lives in a different repository --- but it was found in the same arc, it
contaminated the same gradients, and it had to be removed before the decomposition
measurements could be trusted. It is not independent \emph{in kind}: a guard is a branch, and
Section~\ref{sec:branch} arrives at the same object from the other end. It is also the
cleanest member of the class: root cause named to a line, confirmed by repair.

\subsection{The line}

IDWarp \textbf{2.6.2} is the exact version in \artifact{dafoam/opt-packages:latest}
(image digest
\artifact{sha256:9d45679d55fd\allowbreak 47f5ca7afd99\allowbreak cabb86c7c272\allowbreak 9cf2acf34c43\allowbreak 8eb33af5290f07fc}); the
installed \artifact{idwarp/*.py} files are md5-identical to the \texttt{v2.6.2} tag of
\artifact{https://github.com/mdolab/idwarp}, tag commit \commit{647fd8fc}.

The warp displaces each volume node by an inverse-distance weighted sum in which the weights
\(W_i\) are \emph{constant} with respect to the surface coordinates; all the surface
dependence lives in a rotation matrix \(M_i\) and a displacement \(b_i\), with
\(S_i = M_i\cdot(r - X_{u0,i}) + (X_{u,i} - r)\). \textbf{The rotation acts on the lever arm
from the surface node to the volume node.} \(M_i\) is built at
\artifact{src/modules/kd\_tree.F90:1639--1640} as \(R(n_0 \rightarrow n)\), by:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Fortran]
real(kind=realType), parameter :: tol = 1.4901161193847656e-08   ! line 44 (= sqrt(eps))
call cross_product_3d(v1, v2, axis)
call getMag(axis, axisMag)
! When axisMag is less that sqrt(eps), the acos 'arg' value will be
! exactly one which will give a nan in complex mode.
if (axisMag < tol) then                                          ! line 58   <<< HERE
    angle = zero
    axis  = zero
    axis(1) = one
else
    axis = axis / axisMag
    vv1 = v1 / magv1 ;  vv2 = v2 / magv2
    arg   = min(one, vv1(1)*vv2(1) + vv1(2)*vv2(2) + vv1(3)*vv2(3))
    angle = acos(arg)
end if
Mi = Mi + sin(angle) * A + (one - cos(angle)) * C                ! Rodrigues
\end{lstlisting}

The map \(M_i(n_0, n)\) is perfectly smooth at \(n = n_0\), with the finite, non-zero
derivative \(\mathrm{d}M_i = [n_0\times \mathrm{d}n]_\times\). But \emph{this
parameterisation} --- normalise the cross product, take \texttt{acos} of the dot product,
apply Rodrigues --- has a removable coordinate singularity there: \texttt{axis} is \(0/0\)
and \(\mathrm{d}(\arccos)/\mathrm{d}(\texttt{arg}) \rightarrow \infty\). The guard exists to
stop that becoming a NaN, and the source comment says so. It replaces the singularity with
\texttt{angle = 0}.

\paragraph{What Tapenade does with the guard.} In the generated reverse file
\artifact{src/adjoint/outputReverse/vectorUtils\_b.f90:123--128}, when the guard branch was
taken the reverse sweep \emph{zeroes every adjoint on the path back to} \texttt{v2}; the
subsequent \texttt{GETMAG\_B} and \texttt{CROSS\_PRODUCT\_3D\_B} calls therefore propagate
exactly zero, and \(\mathrm{d}M_i/\mathrm{d}(\texttt{normals}) = 0\) exactly. Tapenade is not
at fault: it differentiated the branch that was taken. The defect is that the branch taken is
a \emph{constant} where the true function has a non-zero first-order term.

\paragraph{The guard fires with certainty at every gradient evaluation.} \artifact{getMag}
regularises with \(+10^{-30}\) under the square root. At an undeformed baseline
\texttt{normals == normals0} bit-for-bit (same code, same inputs), so
\(\texttt{axis} = n_0\times n = 0\) and \(\texttt{axisMag} = \sqrt{10^{-30}} = 10^{-15} <
1.4901\times10^{-8}\). \textbf{Branch 0, guaranteed, at every non-corner surface node.} A
\artifact{check\_totals}, a first design iteration, and every gradient probe are evaluated at
exactly that state. The finite difference, meanwhile, perturbs the surface by \(h\) and tilts
adjacent faces by \(\theta\sim h/L\), far above the threshold --- so the FD sees a
first-order rotation term the AD has set identically to zero. \emph{That difference is the
gap.}

\subsection{Narrowing the claim, against ourselves}

The obvious statement --- ``the rotation term is mis-differentiated'' --- is too broad, and
measurement refuted it. Across five of IDWarp's own regression meshes, run with the
project's own displacements:

\textbf{When the rotation branch is live, \artifact{warpDeriv} is correct.} The catastrophic
failure is confined to \(n \approx n_0\). This is a sharper and more falsifiable statement
than the one we started with, and it is the correct one.

\paragraph{The capstone that could have refuted it.} If the failure is the degenerate branch
specifically, then moving a case off its undeformed baseline should make the guard stop
firing, at which point \artifact{warpDeriv} must become sensitive to \texttt{useRotations}
and get dramatically more accurate. With all 27 shape design variables pre-set to 0.02 on the
U-bend case, the two sign flips vanish and the worst error falls from 207.0\% to 8.2\% with
nothing else changed --- and the fingerprint confirms the branch is now live:
\(\lVert dX_s\rVert\) is \(1.495168856286067\times10^{3}\) at the undeformed baseline whether
rotations are on or off (\emph{identical} --- branch dead), and
\(1.566420804338303\times10^{3}\) vs \(1.495168856286067\times10^{3}\) pre-deformed
(\emph{differs} --- branch live). Had the mechanism been ``rotations are mis-differentiated'',
this experiment would have failed flatly.

\subsection{Why upstream's CI cannot see it}

Four independent mechanisms, each verified against primary source.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The assertion is missing.} \artifact{tests/test\_USMesh.py:86} calls
\artifact{mesh.verifyWarpDeriv(...)} and \emph{discards the result}. Nothing is asserted on
it --- which is exactly what upstream issue \#57 says.
\item \textbf{The only thing asserted is blind by construction.} The regression check is
\texttt{handler.root\_add\_val("... Sum of dxs:", val, tol=1e-8)}. Across five meshes the
rotation term contributes \emph{exactly zero} to that sum (identical to 0--1 ulp) while
changing \(\lVert dX_s\rVert\) by factors of 5 to 34. Five for five, upstream's check cannot
detect a change that alters the derivative vector by a factor of 34.
\item \textbf{A dot-product test cannot detect it either, and is not run.}
\artifact{examples/structured/dotprod.py} lives in \artifact{examples/}, not
\artifact{tests/}. And a dot-product test only proves forward-AD and reverse-AD are mutual
transposes: if both linearise a \emph{different} function than the warp, they do so
identically and the dot product is still zero.
\item \textbf{The verification tool defaults to a random seed.}
\artifact{UnstructuredMesh.py:460}: \texttt{def verifyWarpDeriv(self, dXv=None, ...,
randomSeed=314)}. A random direction contracts the error against a direction it barely
overlaps (0.32--1.30\% where the real objective seed reads 207\%, flipped).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Not a documented approximation.} No documentation, release note, docstring, or
source comment qualifies \artifact{warpDeriv} as approximate. The docs say only ``Compute the
warping derivative (dXv/dXs\textasciicircum T)*Vec''; the \artifact{warpDeriv.F90} header says
only ``Compute the following matrix vector product: dXv/dXs\textasciicircum T * Xvbar''. Both
present it as the exact transpose Jacobian. \emph{One disclosed gap:} the primary IDWarp
reference (Secco, Kenway, He, Mader, Martins, \emph{AIAA J.}\ 59(4):1151--1168, 2021,
doi:10.2514/1.J059491) could not be retrieved --- five umich URLs return 403 including the
authors' own bibliography link, and OpenAlex (\texttt{is\_oa: false}, \texttt{oa\_status:
"closed"}) and Semantic Scholar independently report no open copy. No claim is made about
what that paper says, and the ``undocumented'' characterisation rests on code, docs and
release notes only.

\subsection{The upstream issue this answers}

\artifact{mdolab/idwarp\#57}, ``\texttt{inflate\_cube} test appears to fail'', opened
2021-07-14, labelled \texttt{bug}, \textbf{open, zero comments, five years untouched}. Its
timeline contains exactly one event: the label, applied 8 seconds after creation. Its author
wrote: \emph{``the output from this test appears to show large errors in some of the
derivative terms. However, these error values are not actually used as a unit test and so the
entire test suite still passes''}, and \emph{``Given how weird the FD values are for the two
terms with big errors, it may be that the issue is in those rather than the AD values, but
it's hard to see how that could be the case.''}

We reproduced the issue exactly --- IDWarp alone, no DAFoam, no OpenFOAM, no CFD, using
IDWarp's own test mesh, options, displacement and verification routine --- and answered its
open question adversarially. A step-size study on the two failing degrees of freedom gives FD
values stable to six significant figures across three decades (\(h = 10^{-4}\) to
\(10^{-6}\)), with subtractive roundoff appearing only at \(10^{-8}\).
\textbf{The finite difference is converged; the AD is the wrong one.}

Why degrees of freedom 0 and 3 and not 1, 2, 4, 5: \texttt{inflate\_cube} scales an
axis-aligned block by (1.1, 1.2, 1.3), which leaves every face normal \emph{exactly}
unchanged, so the guard fires even at the deformed state. Perturbing a node \emph{along} its
face normal tilts adjacent faces at first order and switches the rotation term on; perturbing
it \emph{in} the face plane does not. That is a clean instance of our own predicted-clean
control, appearing unbidden inside upstream's five-year-old data.

\subsection{The patch, and what it proves}

The derivation was written \emph{before} the code, and every sign was checked by a controlled
experiment with a hand-computed known answer before any system-level run. With
\(u_1 = v_1/\lvert v_1\rvert\), \(u_2 = v_2/\lvert v_2\rvert\): \(\sin\theta\,[k]_\times =
[u_1\times u_2]_\times\) exactly, and \((1-\cos\theta)[k]_\times^2 = O(\theta^2)\), so
\(M_i = I + [u_1\times u_2]_\times + O(\theta^2)\) and, at \(u_2 = u_1\),

\[
\mathrm{d}M_i = [w]_\times,\quad w = \frac{v_1\times \mathrm{d}v_2}{\lvert v_1\rvert\,\lvert v_2\rvert}
\qquad\text{(forward)}
\]
\[
v_{2b} \mathrel{+}= \frac{\operatorname{axial}(m_{ib} - m_{ib}^{\mathsf T})\times v_1}{\lvert v_1\rvert\,\lvert v_2\rvert}
\qquad\text{(reverse, by the cyclic triple product)}
\]

\(\lvert v_1\rvert\) and \(\lvert v_2\rvert\) are already recomputed in the reverse routine's
forward sweep and are in scope inside the branch. \textbf{The whole fix is four lines of

\paragraph{Two limits, stated plainly.} First, \textbf{retuning the tolerance cannot fix
this.} \texttt{tol} is exactly \(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\), but the \texttt{acos(min(1, v1.v2))}
construction is numerically flat on its own out to \(\sqrt{2\varepsilon} =
2.1073\times10^{-8}\) rad: \(1-v_1\!\cdot\!v_2 = \theta^2/2\) drops below \(\varepsilon\),
\texttt{arg} rounds to bit-exact 1.0, and \texttt{acos(1.0)} is exactly 0, guard or no guard.
Measured at \(\theta = 10^{-8}\): \texttt{arg} bit-exactly 1.0, \(1-\texttt{arg}\) bit-exactly
0.0. \emph{The first thing a maintainer will reach for is foreclosed.} Second, \textbf{the
AD's zero is the exact derivative of the implemented function at that point.} The guard makes
the warp genuinely flat in a ball of angular radius \(\sim\!1.5\times10^{-8}\) rad, and a
finite difference taken entirely inside that ball returns exactly 0.0, agreeing with the AD to
every digit. Tapenade is correct about the code as written. The defect is precisely that
\artifact{warpDeriv} returns \emph{the derivative of a flat spot that no optimizer can
traverse and no finite difference can resolve} --- reaching it needs
\(h < 1.5\times10^{-8}L\), and the step study already shows roundoff destroying the FD at
\(h = 10^{-8}\). Any filing should pre-empt the response ``the code is differentiable and your
step is too coarse.''

\subsection{The second regime, and the fix that covers both}

Away from the baseline, with \texttt{axisMag} just above \texttt{tol}, the guard does not fire
and the derivative \emph{is} computed --- but through an \texttt{acos} whose argument has lost
its information to cancellation. This regime is real, measured on ONERA M6 at \(\sim\)1.26\%,
and \textbf{survives the patch, exactly as predicted} before the acceptance runs. Its cause
was \emph{tested} rather than asserted, by repairing each candidate line in isolation:

The dominant term is \texttt{angle = acos(arg)} in the \emph{primal}: \(\texttt{arg} = 1 -
\theta^2/2\) loses the information at \(\theta\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) before \texttt{acos} is
ever called, and the reverse inherits it. Neither line is sufficient alone and the two do not
add. Only \texttt{angle = atan2(axisMag/(magv1*magv2), arg)} \emph{together with}
\(\sqrt{1-\texttt{arg}^2} \rightarrow \texttt{axisMag}/(\texttt{magv1}\cdot\texttt{magv2})\)
removes the regime --- and the substitute is free, because \texttt{axisMag} is already
computed two lines earlier in the primal.

\textbf{The recommended upstream fix is neither of our patches but the reparameterisation}
\(R = I + [v]_\times + [v]_\times^2/(1+c)\) with \(v = a\times b\), \(c = a\cdot b\): a
polynomial in \(v\) with the smooth coefficient \(1/(1+c)\), giving \(I\) with the correct
derivative at \(a = b\), needing no threshold, and unable to NaN except at exact reversal ---
a genuine singularity of the problem rather than of the parameterisation. It is measured here
at \(6.3\times10^{-13}\) to \(2.2\times10^{-12}\) across every angle tested \emph{including
inside the guard ball}, where every \texttt{acos}-based variant reads exactly 1.0. It is the
only single change that covers both regimes.

Two caveats attach. A regime-2 repair \textbf{cannot} be primal-bit-identical --- it changes
\texttt{angle} in the forward warp (\(M_i\) moves by 1 ulp at \(\theta = 1\)) --- so any such
patch must be re-graded on the primal as well as on the gradient. And our shipped patch is a
\emph{proof of concept on the generated file}: upstream should fix the primal's
parameterisation and regenerate, not merge a hand-edit of Tapenade output.

\subsection{Workaround, with its price named}

\texttt{useRotations: False} in \artifact{meshOptions} restores gradient consistency to
roundoff on all evidence here --- it makes the warp exactly linear in the surface
coordinates. It is not free: upstream documents rotation interpolation as what ``will help
preserve boundary layer orthogonality'' under large orientation changes, so mesh quality
under large deformation is the price. \textbf{That trade has not been measured by this lab},
and no optimisation has been re-run under it. Recommending it as a default would be premature.

\section{The conditioning wall, and the sub-LU remedy}
\label{sec:pc}

\subsection{The wall}

On a 21{,}000-cell curved-backward-facing-step (CBFS) field-inversion case, the DAFoam
adjoint linear solve fails at iteration 0 with \texttt{PetscConvergedReason: -9}
(\texttt{DIVERGED\_NANORINF}) and printed iteration-0 residual
\(7.091590452305\times10^{-4}\). It fails identically under all five available
\artifact{jacMatReOrdering} values --- two produce \(-9\), three produce \(-3\) flat
stagnation, none converges --- and the printed initial residual is identical to 13 digits
across all of them, which is the tell that the failure is in the factorization, not the
Krylov space.

\subsection{The offline reproducer, which made every candidate a ten-second experiment}

The dumped CBFS system (\artifact{dRdWTPC}, \(210{,}592^2\), nnz 13{,}710{,}468, plus the
right-hand side whose norm matches the printed iteration-0 residual to 13 digits) was solved
with a petsc4py harness that replicates \artifact{DALinearEqn::createMLRKSP}'s call sequence
\emph{in order} --- \texttt{KSPSetFromOptions} first, hard-coded GMRES/ASM/sub-ILU settings
second --- under \texttt{mpirun -np 4}, the real layout:

\end{lstlisting}

Bit-for-bit DAFoam's failure --- same reason, same iteration count, same residual --- with
\texttt{PCView} confirming full parity (ILU fill 1, ordering rcm, shift \texttt{NONZERO},
pivot-in-blocks, zeropivot 2.22e-14).

explains, mechanically, the earlier observation that \texttt{spilu} reported ``exactly

every pivot threshold.

\subsection{Where the remedy comes from, and why it is not exotic}

Switching failing ILU sub-blocks to LU is the PETSc developers' own canonical escalation, not
an invention of this lab. From the petsc-users archive, verbatim: \emph{``The default
preconditioner with ILU(0) on each process is not appropriate for your problem and is
producing overflow. Try -sub\_pc\_type lu and see if that produces a different result''}
(Barry Smith, \texttt{msg24474}, 2015-03-26); and \texttt{msg26973} (Hong, 2015-10-27)
demonstrates on a zero-diagonal system that ILU with any shift ``does not converge'' while
\texttt{-sub\_pc\_type lu -sub\_pc\_factor\_shift\_type nonzero} converges in 24 iterations.
The PETSc FAQ states, verbatim: \emph{``The convergence of many of the preconditioners in
PETSc including the default parallel preconditioner block Jacobi depends on the number of
processes.''}

The complete upstream DAFoam remedy ladder for a non-converging adjoint, assembled from every
place it is stated (the run-script guide, the FAQ, the doxygen \texttt{DAOPTION} page, and the
live discussion threads) is: \texttt{renumberMesh -overwrite}; \texttt{pcFillLevel} 1
\(\rightarrow\) 2; \texttt{jacMatReOrdering} rcm \(\rightarrow\) nd/natural;
\texttt{gmresRestart}/\texttt{gmresMaxIters} up; \texttt{asmOverlap} up; upwind schemes;
(since 2025-12) kahip decomposition; and \texttt{transonicPCOption} for transonic cases.
That last rung has a trapdoor of its own, reported in Section~\ref{sec:deadlever}: the value
this lab's transonic scripts carried on it is dead code for the solver they ran, accepted
without a warning and echoed back as though live.
\textbf{Nothing in that ladder changes the sub-PC type; the ILU choice itself is never
questioned upstream}, and no upstream doc, issue or discussion mentions ILU singularity, zero
pivots, or a direct/sub-direct factorization at all --- under 8 targeted searches plus 4
doc-page reads. Meanwhile \artifact{mdolab/dafoam} discussion \#1002, converted to issue
\#1011 on 2026-07-29 and still open, shows users exhausting the documented ladder on
\emph{official tutorials} and stalling: the failure mode this switch was built for is live
upstream today.

\paragraph{The shape of a filing.} An upstream pull request exposing the sub-PC type as an
\artifact{adjEqnOption} key --- default \texttt{ilu}, behaviour unchanged --- would be novel,
is one option plumb, and is backed by both a measured unblock and named-PETSc-developer
precedent. Our local implementation is one hunk at \artifact{DALinearEqn.C:266}
(\artifact{demo-output/website/dafoam/subpclu\_patch/DALinearEqn\_subpclu.patch},
regenerated at \commit{9796f90f}); its default-off behaviour was verified by re-running the
unpatched configuration on the patched image and reproducing the \(-9\) at iteration 0 with
all 13 residual digits intact. \emph{Added 2026-08-07:} the committed diff is now
mechanically applicable. The version this document originally cited carried absolute headers
into a scratch directory that no longer exists, so \texttt{git apply} failed on the very file
cited as the reproduction diff --- filed by a family supervision pass and fixed by
regenerating against the stock \artifact{DALinearEqn.C} extracted from the shipped image with
\artifact{a/src/adjoint/\allowbreak DALinearEqn/DALinearEqn.C} headers.
\textbf{\texttt{git apply --check} now PASSES} from the DAFoam repository root, and the
19-line applied block was verified byte-identical to the change in the deployed
\artifact{dafoam-subpclu:v1} image. One declared delta: the regenerated patch adds a warning
on a set-but-unrecognized \texttt{DAFOAM\_SUBPC\_TYPE} value (\texttt{LU}, \texttt{Lu}, a
trailing space --- all of which previously fell through to stock ILU in silence), and the v1
image is \emph{not} rebuilt, so that warning exists in patch text only and drivers must keep
asserting the sub-LU log line until a rebuild lands.

\subsection{What the change does \emph{not} fix}

On the NASA hump (51{,}626 cells, 51{,}626 \(\beta\) design variables) the same one-word
change moves the failure from catastrophic to honest, and no further. The
\texttt{-9} is gone --- the complete-LU factorization of blocks ILU cannot factor completes,
and GMRES iterates with a monotonically descending residual, \(1.094138002900\) at iteration 0
to \(0.9544468674795\) at iteration 900 --- but it descends slowly, 12.8\% in 900 iterations
(accelerating: 0.75\%, 1.1\%, 1.2\%, 1.6\% per successive hundred) and far from the target.
The run was stopped deliberately at roughly iteration 900 when host available memory fell from
30.5~GB to a low-water \textbf{1.62~GB} under about 19~GB of LU factors plus a still-filling
2000-vector restart space, on a shared box.

Stated for whoever picks this up: \textbf{the hump blocker is no longer the singular
factorization --- it is Krylov convergence rate against a memory envelope}, which is a
different and honestly-named problem.

\emph{Added 2026-08-08.} The same envelope bounds where this remedy can be carried at all.
Taken to a compressible transonic case --- ONERA M6, 21{,}840 cells, np=4, six transported
fields, \(\sim\)38k unknowns per ASM sub-block --- the complete-LU factorization was
OOM-killed at a 10~GB cap and, at 20~GB, held 19.94~GiB resident plus 12~GB of host swap while
completing \emph{zero} hundred-iteration KSP blocks in 27.9 minutes, against the same case's
stock-ILU run at 5{,}876.6~MiB total and 82~s per hundred iterations. Feasibility of the sub-LU
switch is block-size- and field-count-bound, not case-count-bound: CBFS fits, M6 at np=4 does
not. That case turned out not to need it --- Section~\ref{sec:deadlever}.

\section{The option that parsed and did nothing}
\label{sec:deadlever}
%======================================================================

Section~\ref{sec:pc} reported a wall that was where it looked, and a remedy for it. This
section reports the case that remedy was carried to next, where the wall was real and its
cause was not what the record said. The misattribution was this lab's own, in this lab's own
records, and the correction is printed here at the same length as the claim it replaces.

\subsection{The record this section corrects}

From 2026-07-29 the record read as follows. The ONERA M6 family --- compressible, transonic,
\texttt{DARhoSimpleCFoam} --- returns \texttt{PetscConvergedReason: -5}
(\texttt{DIVERGED\_BREAKDOWN}) on \emph{both} adjoint solves at every mesh size tried:
21{,}840, 42{,}120, 79{,}560 and 99{,}840 cells, while an incompressible case of this lab's
converges cleanly at 63{,}920. Memory was excluded --- the smallest failing case peaks at
5.9~GB with more than 20~GB free --- and a dedicated conditioning campaign
(\artifact{R5\_ADJOINT\_CONDITIONING.md}) measured what could be measured about the operator:

\begin{itemize}
\item The assembled preconditioner matrix \artifact{dRdWTPC} (\(200{,}360^2\), 22{,}017{,}324
nonzeros), dumped with a stock \texttt{-ksp\_view\_pmat} and analysed offline, carries a
\textbf{diagonal magnitude spread of \(1.48\times10^{14}\) --- 14.17 decades}, essentially the
dynamic range of a double.
\item \texttt{normalizeResiduals: None} converts the denormal collapse into honest \(-3\)
stagnation, three times, on both objectives independently --- and \emph{worsens} the measured
diagonal spread to 16.14 decades, which is how the record came to name ``a second, still-live
layer of ill-conditioning.''
\item Both attempts to strengthen the preconditioner from that sane baseline ---
\texttt{pcFillLevel} \(0\rightarrow1\), and the literature-precedented nested-Richardson wrap
(\texttt{globalPCIters}/\texttt{localPCIters} \(=3\)) --- reintroduce the collapse, in four
independent solves, at exactly iteration 1000: the GMRES restart boundary.
\item Jacobian colour count does not separate the converging cases from the diverging ones
(1{,}999 colours on a converging incompressible case against 1{,}233--1{,}391 on every
diverging compressible one).
\end{itemize}

The failure also lies about itself, and the record caught that much: when the residual
denormal-collapses toward zero the relative-ratio gate in
\artifact{DALinearEqn::\allowbreak solveLinearEqn} is trivially satisfied, so a \(-5\) run
prints \emph{``Residual tolerance satisfied, solution finished!''} and carries on to the next
objective. Every claim above was measured, is reproducible, and still stands as measured. What
was wrong was the sentence they were assembled into: \emph{the M6 adjoint is blocked by
conditioning at every mesh size}.

\subsection{The lever that was never pulled}

Every archived M6 run --- the ladder logs, all seven R5 campaign logs at line 489, the
reordering pair, the 21{,}840-cell sweep baseline at line 410 --- echoes
\texttt{transonicPCOption 2;} in its own daOptions dump. Read the solver that consumed it,
verbatim from the shipped image:

\begin{lstlisting}
// src/adjoint/DAResidual/DAResidualRhoSimpleCFoam.C:171-176
    // for PC we do not include the div(phid, p) term, this improves the convergence
    if (isPC && daOption_.getOption<label>("transonicPCOption") == 1)
    {
        pEqn -= fvm::div(phid, p_, divPhidPScheme);
    }
\end{lstlisting}

That is the only comparison against \texttt{transonicPCOption} in the file. A branch on
\texttt{== 2} does exist --- at \artifact{DAResidualTurboFoam.C:176}, in a different solver,
doing something else entirely (dropping the off-diagonal terms of the \(\phi\) residual).
\artifact{pyDAFoam.py:396} initialises the option to \(-1\) and documents it, verbatim:
\emph{``Which options to use to improve the adjoint equation convergence of transonic
conditions / This is used only for transonic solvers such as DARhoSimpleCFoam''} --- naming as
its example the solver for which the value our scripts carried does nothing at all.

The value is read through a plain \texttt{getOption<label>} lookup. It is not validated, not
range-checked, and not reported: the run prints \texttt{transonicPCOption 2;} in its settings
echo --- which is a faithful report of the \emph{dictionary}, and says nothing about the
\emph{solver} --- and then takes no branch. So the sentence the record could not write until
2026-08-08: \textbf{no archived M6 adjoint, at any mesh size, ever ran with an active
transonic preconditioner.} The wall was characterised in full with the mitigation built for
exactly that symptom silently off --- and that mitigation is a rung on the upstream remedy
ladder quoted in Section~\ref{sec:pc} (``\texttt{transonicPCOption} for transonic cases''),
the rung a \texttt{DARhoSimpleCFoam} user in trouble is told to try.

\paragraph{The search space, stated, because a deadness claim without one is not a finding.}
This lab learned that rule the expensive way two days later (Section~\ref{sec:audits}), so the
claim above is stated with its own reach attached. In the shipped image,

confirming rung would have been, and it is the answer the rung was launched to get.

The conditional FD arm was correctly not launched --- stage 1 did not converge, so its

170--190 approved, under budget because a pre-registered gate did its job.

\subsection{The second silent configuration: the time-0 rewrite}

The same arc surfaced a second way for a configuration to be silently not what it says. At the
end of a primal, \texttt{pyDAFoam} writes the end state back into the time-0 directory: on this
case \artifact{0/U} came out bit-identical to \artifact{1000/U} (\commit{f77b2607}). Nothing in the log announces
it. Every subsequent run of that case directory therefore starts from the previous run's
converged fields --- and the wrapper's own \texttt{renameSolution} then hard-raises
(\artifact{pyDAFoam.py:1543}) on the leftover \texttt{0.0001} directory it created, so the
natural repair (delete the leftover time directory, rerun) clears the obstacle while leaving
the overwritten time~0 in place. The failure mode is the dangerous kind: the run proceeds, and
the cold start you assumed is not the start that ran.

Caught inside this campaign --- an arm whose ``cold'' primal ran its full 1000 steps in 10~s
from the previous run's converged fields, finishing 2.2\% in \(\lVert U\rVert\) from the
record's own writeout --- it was then audited backwards across the lab's DAFoam record
(\artifact{WARMSTART\_AUDIT.md}, \commit{9bb948b2}; zero solver cost, log forensics only). The
audit's own priced discriminator failed first and is reported as such: ``a warm primal is an
order of magnitude faster'' is \emph{invalid} on this rung --- cold and warm primals both take
\(\sim\)10~s, and the contrast the proposal had priced was coloring-computation time. The
surviving discriminator is the first \texttt{Time step continuity errors} value: cold-from-uniform
\(0.597\) against warm \(0.0107\), a \(55\times\) separation, and bit-reproducible ---
\emph{six} independent cold starts print \texttt{0.5969274433533561} to all 16 digits.

Nine conclusion-bearing records were graded against it. Seven are \textbf{cold-clean}, most of
them by construction: the W4 studies ran each arm in its own staged case copy, which is
structurally immune to the hazard, and their first continuity errors are bit-identical across
arms --- including the reordering ladder and the sub-LU unblock of Section~\ref{sec:pc}, whose
comparisons are therefore provably same-state. Two carry flags, neither load-bearing: a set of
M6 lever nulls whose logs were lost with a session scratchpad (formally indeterminate, mechanism
certain --- and closed on 2026-08-10 by retiring all eleven as superseded rather than rerunning
them: each tested a lever against a preconditioner that was never on, so a cold rerun would have
certified nulls about a configuration no future run will use),
and the R5 matrix dumps quoted at the head of this section, which were assembled about a warm state
\(\sim\)2\% off in \(\lVert U\rVert\) and whose conclusions are order-of-magnitude spreads
(14 decades) that such a drift cannot move. No record-grade conclusion was overturned, and the
audit rewrote none of them; the flags are printed here for the same reason they were printed
there.

the user's verification step agrees with the code rather than with the user.

\paragraph{The detail that makes this a defect and not a documentation gap: the monitors work.}
Viewers and monitors are \emph{not} overridden. \texttt{-ksp\_monitor\_true\_residual},
\texttt{-ksp\_view\_pmat binary:} and \texttt{-ksp\_view\_rhs binary:} all behave exactly as
PETSc documents. A user therefore receives \textbf{correct diagnostics and silently discarded
remedies through the same channel} --- which is the worst available combination, because the
diagnostic path constitutes a working demonstration that the option channel is live. Every
signal available to that user says their command line was received. It was.

This lab is the worked example, twice over, and both halves were measured before the source was
read. The whole diagnosis of Section~\ref{sec:elimination} --- the dumped preconditioner
matrices, the true-residual tracking, the preconditioned \(\kappa=9.57\times10^{10}\) from
\texttt{KSPComputeExtremeSingularValues} --- was obtained \emph{through this option channel},
and every remedy that diagnosis points to is selected through the same channel and discarded by
it. A user who does the hard part correctly is left holding a diagnosis they cannot act on
without recompiling \artifact{libDASolver.so}.

It also sharpens, rather than contradicts, the measurement in
Section~\ref{sec:pc}: factor options such as \texttt{-sub\_pc\_factor\_zeropivot} \emph{do} land
and were proven to land by \texttt{PCView}, because PETSc applies them inside
\texttt{PCSetUp\_ASM} --- after DAFoam's overrides. The boundary is now exact. Options consumed
after line 138 by objects DAFoam does not touch again take effect; options that select the
Krylov method or the preconditioner family do not. ``Compiled-in, nothing reachable'' was too
strong, and ``the option channel works'' is too strong in the other direction; what is true is
that the channel carries diagnostics and drops strategy.

\paragraph{Three graded fixes, cheapest last.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Respect the channel: move \texttt{KSPSetFromOptions} last.} Set the
\artifact{daOptions} values first and call \texttt{KSPSetFromOptions} (plus
\texttt{PCSetFromOptions}) as the final step, which is the ordering PETSc's own examples use.
Then \artifact{daOptions} act as defaults and runtime options as overrides --- what both APIs
promise. Cost: a reorder, plus explicitly re-applying any setting that genuinely must not be
user-overridable, with a comment saying why.
\item \textbf{If the override is intentional, say so, in two places.} One \texttt{Info} line at
setup naming the fixed stack and the ignored options, and the same sentence in the
\artifact{adjEqnOption} reference. This costs nothing and converts a silent failure into a
stated limitation.
\item \textbf{Cheapest partial: namespace the objects.}
\texttt{KSPSetOptionsPrefix(ksp, "dafoam\_")} and the same on the sub-KSPs and PCs, so the
objects never consume unprefixed user options and the boundary stops being invisible.
\end{enumerate}

Severity is \textbf{medium} and the class is what matters rather than the severity: no wrong
number is produced, and everything DAFoam does configure works. It costs nothing until a user
has a hard problem, and then it costs them the ability to act on their own correct analysis, in
a way no log will show. A user of a discrete-adjoint code who reaches for \texttt{-pc\_type
gamg} is, by construction, a user who has already done the hard part.

\subsection{What is reportable here, and what is not}

Three behaviours in this section are properties of the shipped tools rather than of this
lab's cases, and they are the reason it sits in a defect report at all:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A configuration option that parses cleanly and does nothing for the solver
reading it.} \texttt{transonicPCOption: 2} is a legal value --- for
\texttt{DAResidualTurboFoam}. Given to \texttt{DARhoSimpleCFoam} it raises nothing, warns
nothing, and is echoed back in the settings dump exactly as a live option would be. The
information needed to reject it is available at the point of use: the solver knows its own
type. A warning at option-read time on an out-of-range value, or a documented per-solver
applicability table, would have converted nine days of misattributed conditioning work into a
one-line log message. This is not the branch class of Sections~\ref{sec:decomp}--\ref{sec:idwarp}
and it is not a wrong number; it is a usability and diagnosability defect, and its blast
radius is measured: every M6 conclusion in this lab's archive, one of which was wrong.
\item \textbf{A state rewrite with no log line.} The time-0 overwrite is defensible as a
restart convenience and indefensible as a silent one, particularly in a wrapper whose own
rename step then fails on the artifact it just produced. Anyone A/B-testing an archived DAFoam
case is exposed, and the exposure is invisible in the log unless you know to read the first
continuity error.
\item \textbf{An option channel that carries diagnostics and drops strategy}
(Section~\ref{sec:kspoverride}). Of the three this is the one with the largest audience,
because it costs nothing until a user has a hard problem and then costs them exactly the move
their analysis earned. It is also the only one of the three whose evidence is entirely in
upstream's own source rather than in this lab's cases: twelve call sites, a five-minute
reproducer on any \artifact{compute\_totals} case, and three graded fixes of which the cheapest
is one line per object.
\end{enumerate}

None is filed. D4 and D5 have no prepared upstream report of the kind
Sections~\ref{sec:decomp} and~\ref{sec:idwarp} carry --- what exists is the measured record
cited throughout this section --- while D6 has one, complete and \textbf{filing-ready}
(\artifact{DEFECT\_CANDIDATE\_ksp\_options\_override.md}, \commit{f29378d9}), held with
everything else. The shape a filing would take is small in all three cases --- a validation
warning, a log line, and a reorder --- and that smallness is the argument for it.

\subsection{Scope, stated against ourselves}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Two rungs, and then a ceiling.} 21{,}840 and 42{,}120 cells both converge and

%======================================================================
\section{Methods: adversarial verification as practice}
\label{sec:methods}
%======================================================================

Every headline claim in this document has survived an independent adversarial sweep. That
phrase is used precisely, and this section says what it buys, because a reader assessing
seven unfiled defect claims is entitled to know what standard they were held to.

\subsection{What a supervisor sweep is}

A verification agent is dispatched with the posture that \textbf{the claim is wrong until it
survives attack}. It does not review the record; it re-derives the record. Concretely, across
the six sweeps behind this document:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Independent instruments, not re-runs of the lab's scripts.} The mechanism sweep
rebuilt the processor-to-serial permutation from scratch with its own parsers, from
\artifact{cellProcAddressing}/\artifact{faceProcAddressing} and the \artifact{AdjointIndexing}
dumps, then compared against the stored map: identical bit-for-bit in target and sign. The
rotation sweep wrote its own NumPy check of the corrected derivative (250 draws, against
Richardson central differences of the singularity-free reference) and its own DAFoam driver
that wraps \artifact{idwarp.USMesh.warpDeriv} itself rather than the lab's hook.
\item \textbf{A new validation channel the original never used.} The AdjointIndexing dumps
carry cell and face centre coordinates; mapping np=4 coordinates onto np=1 coordinates gives
max deviation \textbf{0.0} over all 26{,}149 and 26{,}227 entries, validating every entry of
the permutation exactly and independently of any solved field.
\item \textbf{Re-measurement from scratch, in the container.} One cell of the phenomenon table
(np=2, patched) was re-run with the sweep's own driver, own staging, own container
invocation: analytic, FD, absolute error, relative error, baseline \(C_D\) and GMRES iteration
count \emph{all identical to every printed digit}, and carrying the provenance stamp the
archived run lacked. Cost: 86~s wall, 2.9 solver core-min.
\item \textbf{A point the lab did not choose.} The conditioning sweep verified a
finite-difference cell (6490) the lab never published, at a different rank band, and got
0.0211\% --- tighter than any published cell. A wrong indexing, a stale gradient file, or a
lucky triple would not survive that.
\item \textbf{Cold reproduction of the control, not just the treatment.} The env-off
regression was re-run cold by the sweep, on a fresh case copy: reason, iteration count, and
all 13 residual digits matched independently of anything the original session wrote.
\item \textbf{Recomputation from raw vectors, trusting no staged intermediate.} The robustness
sweep recomputed both of Table~\ref{tab:levers}'s cross-residuals with its own NumPy from the
dumped vectors (1.354841e-02 and 1.047165e+00), rebuilt the np4\(\rightarrow\)np1 permutation
from the raw addressing files --- its reconstruction equals the staged input under
\texttt{np.array\_equal}, for both levers --- verified the sign convention rather than
assuming it, and confirmed \emph{in code} that the instrument's operator is the matrix-free
AD product the KSP uses and never the assembled matrix dumped alongside it.
\item \textbf{A byte-level audit of the ``one word'' claims, because they are load-bearing.}
\texttt{diff} of the R6b case against the established source: one changed line, in
\artifact{fvSchemes}, everything else \texttt{cmp}-clean. \texttt{diff -r} of the serial arm
against the clean case it was built from: the single differing input file is
\artifact{system/fvSchemes}, two lines; and its one-word control differs by exactly one word.
\item \textbf{A free check the record never ran, found by attacking the record's own logic.}
The serial-limiter sweep judged the record's step-stability test logically insufficient
against a symmetric FD kink --- correctly --- and then closed the hole at zero cost by mining
the one-sided slopes out of the FD legs' own printed converged \(C_D\) values
(Table~\ref{tab:onesided}), first validating the reconstruction by rebuilding the printed
central-difference vector from the same values to \(4.7\times10^{-9}\).
\item \textbf{The ledger is audited to the second.} Billed walls were reconciled against log
mtimes and internal timings --- e.g.\ five offline runs plus a 600~s kill summing to 693.04~s
against 693 billed; a memory trace whose first and last samples bracket 816~s against 819
billed; nine finite-difference runs whose per-run walls sum to \emph{exactly} 642; and 21
robustness-campaign lines re-summing to exactly 128.28 with all 21 per-line
\texttt{wall}\(\times\)\texttt{cpus} arithmetic checks passing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Two audits larger than a sweep --- one of which was itself wrong}
\label{sec:audits}

A supervisor sweep re-derives one claim. Twice in this arc a single finding was large enough
to force a sweep of the \emph{archive}, and both are reported with their negative results
because that is what an audit is for. One of the two then produced a false positive of its own,
which is reported here at more length than its successes.

\textbf{The dead-lever audit} (\artifact{DEAD\_LEVER\_AUDIT\_2026-08-08.md},
\commit{946e4a26}) ran the D4 question against every conclusion-bearing record the lab holds:
each sentence leaning on a named solver option, and each such option hunted in the archived
\emph{runtime} logs rather than the input dictionaries. Zero solver cost, read-only. Result:
about 125 distinct lever/conclusion pairs \textbf{verified} against a quoted log line, 16
reported \textbf{unverifiable-from-logs} (the logs are gone or never printed the banner --- and
per the charter those conclusions now carry that caveat on their faces), and four
\textbf{found dead}. Only one of the four reopened a conclusion, and it is D4 itself. The
headline is a null: \emph{no new conclusion-reopening dead lever was found anywhere in the
archive}. That null is worth more than the finding that prompted it, and it is the reason D4 is
reported as one specimen rather than as a pattern in this lab's work.

\textbf{One of those four found-dead verdicts was itself manufactured, and it is the more
instructive half of the audit.} On 2026-08-10 a container-side re-read refuted the entry
recording that \texttt{DASimpleFoam} silently ignores \texttt{consistent yes}: SIMPLEC
\emph{is} implemented, in the primal at \artifact{pEqnSimple.H:27} and in the adjoint at
\artifact{DAResidualSimpleFoam.C:189}, both carrying the textbook
\texttt{rAtU = 1/(1/rAU - UEqn.H1())} form, in both images. The original search
scanned \artifact{DASimpleFoam.C} and \artifact{DASolver.C} and found only two unrelated
comments --- \textbf{the file list, not the search string, was the defect}: the equations live

recorded rather than absorbed: that false deadness had already been adopted as the stated cause

worse than never having explained it, because the search for the real cause stopped for weeks.
A false \textbf{found-dead} is not a missing finding; it is an invented one, and it propagates.
D4's own verdict was re-verified against that standard, with its search space printed on its

\textbf{The warm-start audit} (\artifact{WARMSTART\_AUDIT.md}, \commit{9bb948b2}) did the same

\subsection{The lessons this arc produced}

The method itself is on the record, as numbered lessons in \artifact{LESSONS.md}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{L-29.} A guard that replaces a removable singularity with a constant
differentiates to a hard zero --- at exactly the point every gradient evaluation uses. Audit
every such guard on a differentiated path; require the guarded branch to carry the analytic
limit, or reparameterise so no guard is needed. And when \emph{testing} derivative code,
evaluate at the degenerate/baseline point deliberately and seed with the real objective
direction.
\item \textbf{L-30.} A gate whose threshold lies between two constraint values reports the
constraint, not the quantity. Before gating on a reported extremum, find out whether the tool
was \emph{told} that number.
\item \textbf{L-31.} A verification instrument must itself be verified at the component it
condemns. When an analytic derivative disagrees with a finite difference, re-measure the
finite difference by an independent path --- with a neighbouring component as a control ---
before attributing the gap to the derivative.
\item \textbf{L-32.} When a verdict moves, the case's own record must move first; satellites
are not the record.
\item \textbf{L-33.} A correction trained inside a gated regime rebuilds the gate's own
failure at the regime's floor.
\item \textbf{L-34.} ``Unreachable at runtime'' is a claim about the deployed object, not
about one call site. Deploy a probe that prints the final object state (for PETSc,
\texttt{PCView} on the sub-PC after setup) before declaring a configuration layer unreachable.
\item \textbf{L-35.} \emph{A converged Krylov solve certifies the operator it was given, not
the operator you meant.} When an iterative solve converges cleanly and the downstream answer
is still wrong, stop tuning the solve and test the operator: apply the converged solution
under an independently evaluated instance of the operator --- serial where the suspect is
parallel, assembled where the suspect is matrix-free --- and compare the residual to that
instance's own floor. It is a mat-vec, not a solve: seconds, not core-minutes. Two footnotes:
map vectors across decompositions with the integer addressing files (sign on flipped faces),
never coordinate matching, and validate the map on the primal state first; and diff operator
\emph{actions} on mapped vectors, never operator \emph{entries}.
\item \textbf{L-36.} \emph{A gradient check certifies a contraction, not an operator} --- and
the two do not scale together. Passing \artifact{check\_totals} at one configuration certifies
the contraction of the operator's error with that one objective's adjoint direction: almost no
information about the operator, none about other objectives, other design variables, or other
decompositions. The converse holds too: a large measured operator defect does not imply the
published gradients are badly wrong. Never extrapolate a passing gradient check across
configurations; when an operator defect is suspected, measure the operator.
\item \textbf{L-37.} When a differentiated code is wrong in parallel, \emph{suspect a recorded
branch first} --- limiters, switching or mixed boundary conditions, guards,
\texttt{min}/\texttt{max}/\texttt{abs} --- and one-knob them before theorizing about halo
exchange in general, because a branch is removable by a one-line dictionary edit and is
therefore the cheapest discriminator available. And any lever that turns a gradient error off
must be re-verified at \emph{operator} level before the configuration is described as
unaffected: a lever can fix the operator or merely rotate the contraction away from the
damage, and the gradient number cannot tell them apart. State which one you measured.
\item \textbf{L-38.} \emph{An invariance check can pass because both sides are wrong
together.} Decomposition-, ordering-, rank-count- and restart-invariance detect
\emph{inconsistency} between two evaluations of the same tape; a tape wrong identically in both
sails through all of them. Never promote an invariance pass to a correctness verdict unless one
side is anchored to an external reference. And the converse of L-37's trigger: a recorded
branch can corrupt the reverse sweep in \emph{serial}, with the parallel machinery innocent ---
so when a serial gradient check fails against a step-stable finite difference, put the tape's
branches at the top of the suspect list there too, before FD-quality theories.
\item \textbf{L-40.} \emph{The switch you set is not the switch that ran.} Any option, flag,
model choice or lever that is load-bearing for a conclusion must have its activity confirmed in
the \emph{runtime log} --- not its presence in the input dictionary --- before the conclusion
ships. ``Configured'' and ``active'' are different claims and only the second is evidence: a
dictionary entry can be dead code for the solver that executed it, and the settings echo will
print it back either way. Where the archived log cannot settle it, the conclusion ships marked
\emph{unverifiable-from-logs} rather than quietly assumed. The companion rule from the same arc:
a mesh entering an archive, a pre-registration or a ladder rung carries its \texttt{checkMesh}
record from creation --- born clean or it does not enter.
\item \textbf{L-43, second corollary.} \emph{A found-dead verdict is a claim about
implementation, and implementation hides in include files.} Where L-40 says a lever is evidence
only when the log proves it ran, this is the failure in the opposite direction and it does more
damage: a false ``the code ignores this'' is not a missing finding but an invented one, and it
propagates into whatever it was used to explain. A deadness claim about C++ requires the
build's view rather than a file's --- the whole source tree, \texttt{.H} includes and derived
classes included --- and any verdict of the form ``the code ignores X'' is downgraded to ``not
found in the files searched, which were \ldots'' unless the search space is stated. A negative
claim without its search space is not a finding.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The cross-residual protocol}

Pure DAFoam public API; no rebuild required. This is the instrument any candidate fix must
satisfy.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# 1. Per decomposition: run the primal + adjoint, then dump
#      psi   = DAFoamSolver.psi
#      state = the converged primal state vector
#      b     = dFdW (the adjoint RHS)
#    with, in daOptions:
#      "writeJacobians": ["adjointIndexing"]
#    which emits AdjointIndexing_<rank>_of_<n>.txt (indices + cell/face centres).

# 2. Build the exact integer permutation to serial ordering from
#      processor*/constant/polyMesh/cellProcAddressing
#      processor*/constant/polyMesh/faceProcAddressing   (sign => flipped face)
#    NEVER by coordinate matching. Validate it on the PRIMAL state first:
#    duplicated processor-face phi copies must agree to machine precision
#    (measured here: 2.7e-15). The AdjointIndexing centres give a second,
#    exact channel: max |dxyz| must be 0.0.

# 3. Evaluate the true residual under the SERIAL operator, matrix-free:
#      r = solverAD.calcJacTVecProduct(state -> residual)  applied to mapped psi
#    SIGN CONVENTION: OpenMDAO seeds -dF/dW, so the system is  A^T psi = -b.
#    An instrument computing (A^T psi - b) prints a degenerate ratio of exactly
#    2.0 for its own-operator control -- that is the tell that the sign is wrong.
#    Correct offline as res + 2b, or -- better, and done here on 2026-08-07 --
#    fix the instrument: both reused tasks now compute (A^T psi + b), every log
#    line prints a convention banner, and the fixed task writes a distinct
#    w4xf_res_*.npy prefix so pre-fix dumps (needing +2b) cannot be conflated
#    with true residuals. The repaired tool reproduces the np=1 floor
#    1.140697e-04 with NO offline correction, to every digit.

# 4. Judge against the SERIAL operator's own floor, not against zero:
#      np=1 control ......... 1.14e-04 of ||b||   <- the floor
#      np=4 simple 4x1x1 .... 9.37e-02
#      np=4 scotch .......... 3.288e+02           <- the defect
\end{lstlisting}

instrumented rebuild of \artifact{libDASolver.so}; what has changed is that the reproducer to
instrument can now be a single process (Section~\ref{sec:serial}).
\item \textbf{D1, reproducer on a stock upstream tutorial.} The measured case is lab-built
--- its mesh recipe and case directories are archived and shippable, but it is not an official
tutorial. A tutorial-based reproducer varying the \artifact{decomposeParDict} daOption on an
official case should exist before filing.
\item \textbf{D1, the one-mesh-family confound.} Not repaired --- \emph{confirmed}, twice, by
the two sharpest tests available: the BC alone transplanted onto a clean case from another mesh
family does nothing, and both ingredients together do nothing either. Every decomposition-borne
measurement in this lab still lives on one 2{,}777-cell Ahmed background family. A filed
reproducer must scope itself accordingly.
\item \textbf{D1, the unseparated third correlate.} \artifact{primalMinResTol} is 1e-4 on both
defective cases and 1e-6/1e-8 on all five clean ones, and it has no within-case control,
because DAFoam treats an unreached tolerance as a primal failure rather than a depth setting.
Whether a loose primal is a necessary co-ingredient is unmeasured, and the acquisition null
raised rather than lowered its standing.
\item \textbf{D1, the unbought operator control.} No state-override control was run for either
lever of Table~\ref{tab:levers}; the linearization-state confound is bounded indirectly and the
\(\sim\)0.4 core-min direct control should be taken next time a container is warm.
\item \textbf{D1s, novelty.} The community sweep predates the limiter findings and has not
been re-run for them. No priority is claimed, and the gap should close before any filing.
\item \textbf{D2, the primary reference.} The IDWarp AIAA paper is unread and unobtainable
from this host; the ``undocumented'' characterisation rests on code, docs and release notes
only, and is stated conditionally throughout.
\item \textbf{D2, the second regime.} Real, measured at \(\sim\)1.26\% on ONERA M6, untouched
by the shipped patch by design. The reparameterisation that subsumes both regimes is derived
and numerically validated but not implemented, and cannot be primal-bit-identical.
\item \textbf{D2, the workaround's price.} The mesh-quality cost of \texttt{useRotations:
False} under large deformation has not been measured.
\item \textbf{D3, the hump.} Krylov convergence rate against a memory envelope. A prepared
configuration wrapping the exact sub-solve in Richardson with a capped restart is staged but
unrun.
\item \textbf{D4, the ceiling is bracketed and not located.} Two rungs are verified
(21{,}840 and 42{,}120 cells); 79{,}560 stagnates on ample memory. No mesh between those two
sizes has been run, and one arm at an intermediate size would halve the bracket for an
estimated 40--60 core-min. 99{,}840 cells and above are untouched. Nothing is claimed about
whether a different configuration reaches 79{,}560 --- the named and unrun candidate is the
restart lever (\texttt{gmresRestart} \(200\rightarrow1000\)), whose mechanism, retaining
search directions instead of discarding them twenty times, matches the observed failure mode
and whose memory cost that rung can afford.
\item \textbf{D4, the gradient's unverified components.} At rung 1 two probed components carry
no verdict (\texttt{twist[1]}, \texttt{shape[5]}, both step-inconsistent at the tested steps);
rung 2's three are all evaluable, which locates that failure in the small signals rather than
in the gradient but does not retroactively verify rung 1's two. The remaining components of
both vectors are unprobed, and no optimization has been run on either gradient. The primal
reaches its plateau below the \(10^{-4}\) hard gate without attaining the requested
\(10^{-8}\), which is disclosed on the FD arms' faces.
\item \textbf{D4, the mechanism.} \emph{Why} dropping \(\mathrm{div}(\phi_d,p)\) from the
preconditioner's pressure equation converts breakdown into convergence on this case is not
demonstrated here --- only that it does, reproducibly, at two rungs and against a control. The
account in Section~\ref{sec:artifact} of why strengthening then helps is a plausibility
argument consistent with the measurements, not a derivation. The 14-decade diagonal spread of
the assembled PC matrix has not been re-measured with the option active, and that measurement
is cheap.
\item \textbf{D4, the restart hypothesis.} The mechanism offered for why strengthening the
preconditioner helps at one rung and collapses at the next --- that it destabilises the GMRES
restart recomputation, and that whether this bites depends on whether convergence completes
inside the first restart cycle --- has two supporting points and is \textbf{unproven}. Its
falsifiable prediction is that fill~1 also collapses at 42{,}120 cells. That arm is cheap and
has not been run.
\item \textbf{D4, the mechanism behind the ceiling, is unknown and stays unknown.} Ten
candidate causes are eliminated by measurement (Section~\ref{sec:elimination}) and no
eleventh is named. \textbf{Ten eliminations are not a proof of anything}; they bound the
ignorance rather than resolving it, and the surviving characterisation is negative:
\(\kappa\approx10^{11}\) after preconditioning, insensitive to every parameter the build
exposes. The non-normality measurement carries its own limit --- it is taken on the assembled
\artifact{dRdWTPC}, not on the preconditioned operator whose normality governs GMRES, which this
build never assembles --- so it refutes a discriminator without certifying the operator.
\item \textbf{D6, unmeasured on the fixes.} The three graded fixes are drafted against the
source and none has been implemented or tested; the claim that reordering
\texttt{KSPSetFromOptions} is behaviour-preserving for existing cases is an argument, not a
measurement.
\item \textbf{D4, the two negative signatures.} Whether the stagnation at rung 3 and the
denormal breakdown at the archived rungs share a mechanism is not established; they are
different signatures and no arm here identifies either.
\item \textbf{D4/D5, the unverifiable levers.} The dead-lever audit left 16 lever/conclusion
pairs whose activity no surviving log can settle; per the charter they ship marked
unverifiable-from-logs rather than assumed. Separately, the 11 warm-flagged M6 variant nulls
were closed on 2026-08-10 by \emph{retirement} rather than by rerun: each tested a lever
against a preconditioner that was never on, so a cold rerun would certify nulls about a
superseded configuration. The audit's cold-rerun-before-citation requirement is satisfied by
never citing them, which is the stricter option and is stated as such at both sites.
\item \textbf{D5, upstream's intent.} Whether the time-0 rewrite is deliberate (a restart
convenience) or incidental is not established from the source here; the finding as stated is
about its silence, not its existence.
\end{enumerate}
