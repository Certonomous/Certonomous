The failing row is the most informative one. An eight-decade step-size sweep proved the 11.43% is a real gradient defect, not a checking artefact — one component is sign-inverted at every step size and carries 74–83% of the error. The result independently reproduced a prior investigation to four significant figures. It also retired our own grading standard: a band inferred from a single case was too loose, and a passing verdict elsewhere was downgraded when the evidence improved.

Both failures were diagnosed rather than filed. The duct failure is structural: linear turbulence models produce exactly zero secondary flow — machine zero, confirmed across five models. Reynolds-stress models then bracketed the DNS value at 55% and 207%, proving the mechanism is reachable. The dam-break failure turned out to be the measurement, not the physics: the front-position definition was mesh-dependent and swung the answer by more than 40 percentage points. The gate stays failed until the comparison itself is rebuilt.

4 · Uncertainty quantification on a known model error

The NASA hump has a documented, published model-form error. The test: does our uncertainty band contain it — without being tuned to?

The honest reading. The band contains the error, and that was predicted in writing before the runs. But it is wide — a factor of 2.4 — and the containment is carried by physics-based anisotropy bounds, not by sampling several turbulence models. Sampling members of one model class does not sample the error of the class itself: an inter-model band alone would have missed the truth entirely. Containment is necessary, not sufficient; the open question is how tight the band can be made while still containing.
