1 · Active ML turbulence-closure challenge

A public, ongoing research benchmark with a strict rule: training or validating on any test case withdraws the submission. Each version scored once, in a single pre-registered call — six calls total across five rounds of refinement. As of round 5 (2026-08-07) the entry of record scores 0.0566 — rank 1 of 5, scored locally at benchmark commit deb91557: a local scoring, not an official leaderboard placement; nothing has been submitted.

P(rank 1) = 68%. A case-level bootstrap over the eight scored cases puts the probability that this ordering survives a comparable set of cases at 68% — and an eight-case sample cannot pin that tighter than 2–100% at 95%. The point estimate is 68%; what eight cases actually support is “somewhere between a coin flip and near-certain”.

The lead is not statistically decided. Our 0.002878 margin over Reissmann sits against a per-case spread five times larger, so on a different set of eight cases the ordering could reverse; the same is true of the margin over Wu and Zhang. The margins over Liu and Montoya do survive that test. The standing is two cases wide — on the eight-case mean, alpha_15_13929_2024 alone supplies more than the whole margin, and NASA_2DWMH alone costs more than it. AR_1_Ret_360 and AR_3_Ret_360 are ties below the precision the board publishes to, and are not per-case wins.

What makes the score defensible. Every scoring call is a single call, pre-registered before the predictions it scores were generated. Train, validation and test splits held disjoint and verified. The features needed for two of the four flow families are not shipped by the benchmark — they were reconstructed from raw mesh geometry and validated against the shipped fields on training cases only, agreeing to r = 0.9997–1.0000. On two cases the trained correction was found, honestly, to make the result worse than the uncorrected physics solve; rather than quietly discard that finding, a small decline-gate was trained and checked on non-test cases only, then applied once — it correctly withholds the correction on exactly those two cases. In round 5 the trained duct correction was replaced outright by the untrained QCR2000 constitutive term under a rule frozen before any solve. Two caveats travel with the rank: the 0.0029 margin over the published leader carries a truth-free seed-uncertainty bound of comparable size (0.0024) on the three trained predictions, and the rank is local — the steward's own scoring, if the entry is ever submitted, is the number that counts.

Why this is the important one. A model that is confidently wrong is worse than one that says how wrong it might be. The prediction that our band would contain this error was written down before the runs that tested it, so it could not be fitted after the fact, and the band does contain it. This result stands on one case; it has not yet been tested on a second.

We audited our own headline and it did not survive intact. We re-checked every run behind this result against the convergence standard the solver itself enforces, rather than against the residual numbers that sit next to it and read lower. Two of the runs that set the band's lower edge had never met that standard. One of them was not a flow solution at all: its wall-shear trace oscillates across the entire domain and the number we had published was simply the first crossing a script happened to find inside its search window. Both are withdrawn.

What is left is better than what we published. Keeping only runs that met the standard, the band runs 1.0722 to 1.2534 and still contains the experiment — and it is half as wide as the band we first reported. The extra width had come from the two runs that never converged. Containment now rests on comparing turbulence models against each other, which is the plainer of the two mechanisms and the one that survives scrutiny. The corrections are recorded in full, including the two we found by checking claims we liked.
