the last 400 samples gives **R² = 0.9959 to 0.999996** on the six arms, and τ is stable

**(b) The extrapolation.** For a geometric increment sequence with `r = exp(−1/τ)` the

to the next multiple of 200. The **conservative** reading is used at every level: the
larger of the two arms' ptp and the largest τ over the three fit windows and both arms.

| L2 | 1800 | 0.01205923 K | 333.29 | 1597.3 | 999.9 | 4397.1 | **4400** |

## 3. LIMB A'S READER IS REBUILT — the defect, the repair, and the drive

**R1's `logview_gpu()` was broken in two ways on this build's real PETSc output, and one
of them was a silent FALSE PASS.** Found by the supervisor personally, reading the frozen
file. Both are reproduced on the frozen bytes in `LOGVIEW_REPAIR_DRIVE.txt`.

**(i) The false pass.** R1 took `gpu_pctf` as `max()` of the **last four numeric tokens**
of a `MatMult`/`KSPSolve` row. The real trailing columns are
`GPU Mflop/s | CpuToGpu Count | CpuToGpu Size | GpuToCpu Count | GpuToCpu Size | GPU %F`,
so that `max()` returns the **CpuToGpu SIZE IN MBYTES** whenever it exceeds 100 —
**measured 210.0 / 672.0 / 2320.0** at L1/L2/L3, values that **scale with mesh** and
exceed 100, which a percentage cannot. Clause A3's floor of 99.0 was therefore cleared
**by a megabyte count**. The true GPU %F is **100** on every `MatMult` and `KSPSolve` row.

**(ii) The unpassable clause.** R1's `h2d` regex
`CpuToGpu (?:Count|- CopyTo)\s*[:=]?\s*(\d+)` needs digits immediately after the optional
colon, and the **only** line in the file carrying that phrase is **the legend**, whose
colon is followed by descriptive text. `h2d` was therefore always `None` → returned as 0,
and clause A4 (`h2d <= 0` refuses) **was unpassable on any run, by construction**. The
forced-CPU control read 0/0 correctly **only by coincidence** — its trailing four tokens
happen to all be zero on this build. *A control that is right by accident is not a
control.*

**This is the THIRD time this family has frozen a fixture-shaped parser that meets real
output and misbehaves** — R1 Amendment 1's y+ reader, VMFLGPU002 Amendment 5's limb-A
tell, and now this.

### The repair

Columns are located **by position against the table's own two header lines**, by **two
independent derivations that must agree or the comparator refuses**:

- **D1 — character-span association.** Each token of the sub-header line is assigned to
  the top-header token whose character span it overlaps, ignoring the pure-punctuation
  rules (`-`, `---`). That names every data column: `('CpuToGpu','Count')`, `('GPU','%F')`.
- **D2 — the legend order.** PETSc prints, immediately above the header, one line per
  trailing column naming it. The last seven sub-header tokens must be the terminal words
  of those seven names, in that order. **The legend is used ONLY to name columns and never
  to read a value** — R1's h2d defect was a value-reading regex whose only match in the
  whole file *was* this legend.

If D1 and D2 disagree on either column, the comparator **refuses** (clause A1X). A header
with no `GPU/%F` or no `CpuToGpu/Count` column **refuses** (A1H). A mangled legend
**refuses** (A1L). There is **no fallback to counting tokens from the end of a row.**

Aggregation also **tightens**: R1 gated on `max(GPU %F)` over the qualifying rows, so one
good row could carry a table full of bad ones. R2 gates on the **minimum** over those
rows, and on the **maximum** for the forced-CPU control's leak test. *A tightening is
disclosed as a tightening; it is not presented as a repair.*

### The new refusal that makes the false pass impossible to repeat

**`GPU_PCTF_MAX = 100.0`. GPU %F is a percentage; a value above 100 REFUSES (clause A1P)
rather than clearing the floor.** Had this existed in R1, 210.0 could not have been read
as a pass. It only **adds** a refusal: nothing that refused in R1 passes here.

### The repair is DRIVEN on the real frozen bytes, both arms

`LOGVIEW_REPAIR_DRIVE.txt`, with the six logs' sha256 recorded. R1's comparator is
verified at `blob:a4632b7ac2ef88fd98d14015b614ab4772ae71df` == its hash on disk — **these
are the bytes that graded R1** — and is executed from its bytes rather than imported, so
no bytecode cache stands between the frozen blob and what runs.
