What makes the score defensible. Every scoring call is a single call, pre-registered before the predictions it scores were generated. Train, validation and test splits held disjoint and verified. The features needed for two of the four flow families are not shipped by the benchmark — they were reconstructed from raw mesh geometry and validated against the shipped fields on training cases only, agreeing to r = 0.9997–1.0000. On two cases the trained correction was found, honestly, to make the result worse than the uncorrected physics solve; rather than quietly discard that finding, a small decline-gate was trained and checked on non-test cases only, then applied once — it correctly withholds the correction on exactly those two cases. In round 5 the trained duct correction was replaced outright by the untrained QCR2000 constitutive term — Spalart (2000), run at its published Ccr1 = 0.3 and overridden nowhere — under a rule frozen before any solve. Two caveats travel with the rank. First, the seed uncertainty is larger than the margin, not comparable to it. Against Yang, who leads the six-entry board retrieved 2026-08-11T23:33Z and re-verified unchanged by read-only fetch 2026-08-14T21:01Z, the margin is 0.0014 while the truth-free seed-uncertainty bound on the three trained predictions is 0.0024 — 1.8× the margin. Loaded adversely that bound puts our overall at 0.059047 against Yang's 0.058013, so its adverse end loses the point lead outright: the lead ahead of Yang is not distinguishable from single-seed noise. Second, the rank is local — the steward's own scoring, if the entry is ever submitted, is the number that counts.

the 0.0029 margin over the published leader carries a truth-free seed-uncertainty bound of comparable size (0.0024) — struck 2026-08-14. 0.0029 was the margin over Reissmann on the four-entry board frozen at benchmark commit deb91557 (2026-05-04), where 0.0024 is 0.83× it and “comparable” was fair. Why this is the important one. A model that is confidently wrong is worse than one that says how wrong it might be. The prediction that our band would contain this error was written down before the runs that tested it, so it could not be fitted after the fact, and the band does contain it. This result stands on one case; it has not yet been tested on a second.

We audited our own headline and it did not survive intact. We re-checked every run behind this result against the convergence standard the solver itself enforces, rather than against the residual numbers that sit next to it and read lower. Two of the runs that set the band's lower edge had never met that standard. One of them was not a flow solution at all: its wall-shear trace oscillates across the entire domain and the number we had published was simply the first crossing a script happened to find inside its search window. Both are withdrawn.

What is left is better than what we published. Keeping only runs that met the standard, the band runs 1.0722 to 1.2534 and still contains the experiment — and it is half as wide as the band we first reported. The extra width had come from the two runs that never converged. Containment now rests on comparing turbulence models against each other, which is the plainer of the two mechanisms and the one that survives scrutiny. The corrections are recorded in full, including the two we found by checking claims we liked.
