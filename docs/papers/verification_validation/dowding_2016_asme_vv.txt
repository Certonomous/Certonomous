V&V has the goal of providing evidence for the
credibility of computational simulation
• Computational simulation is (relatively) inexpensive and has
advanced to the point that complex systems can be analyzed
• While computational simulation can be applied to complex
systems, the accuracy of the results are often unknown
– Computer codes to solve the simulation models may have bugs or algorithm
deficiencies (code verification)
– Computational simulations approximate the solution of the mathematical
equations (solution verification)
» Discretization dependencies
– The mathematical equations solved in a computational simulation (PDEs
usually) are an approximation at some level (validation)
» May be based on fundamental principles, but typically contain
constitutive models or “sub-grid” models that are empirically based
» There are simplifications and assumptions in developing the
computation simulation (validation)

4

Definitions of Verification
• Verification
– The process of determining that a computational model accurately
represents the underlying mathematical model and its solution. (V&V10, high level document for solid mechanics)
– Code verification (V&V-20): “Establishes that the code accurately
solves the mathematical model incorporated in the code, i.e. that the
code is free of mistakes for the simulations of interest.”
– Solution verification (V&V-20): Estimates the numerical accuracy of a
particular calculation.

• Code Verification precedes validation
– Establishes that the code solutions are an accurate representation of
the mathematical models
– Goal is finding and removing mistakes in the source code and
numerical algorithms

• Solution (Calculation) Verification performed as part of
validation
– Assuring the numerical accuracy of code output for the problem of
interest
5

Definition of Validation
• Definition Validation (ASME V&V-10/V&V-20):
“The process of determining the degree to which a
model is an accurate representation of the real
world from the perspective of the intended uses of
the model.”
• Key points:
– “Process” (that may be iterative)
– “degree .. accurate representation of real world” (quantitative)
– Intended uses (adequacy)

6

Code Verification
• Code verification should be performed prior to validation
• Establishes that the code solutions are an accurate
representation of the mathematical equations in the simulation
– Goal is finding and removing mistakes in the source code and numerical algorithms

• Performed by comparing analytical solutions of mathematical
equations to simulation code solutions
– Method of Manufactured solutions (MMS) (see Roach, 1997 or Knupp and
Salari, 2003)
– Classical analytical solutions (Beck)

• Simple (conceptually) for single physics (equation) models
– NS for Laminar flow, Nonlinear heat conduction, etc.

• Challenges exist for verifying the fully-coupled mathematical
equations
– CFD for turbulent reacting flow with participating media
– Nonlinear heat conduction with enclosure radiation and chemical kinetics

Numerical solution uncertainty
(unum)
• Code verification needs to be conducted for the
simulation capabilities used in a validation activity
– The evidence for trusting that the simulation code is accurately solving
the mathematical equations
– Code verification does not directly contribute to unum

• Numerical uncertainty unum estimated with Grid
convergence index (GCI)
• Requires running the simulation model while refining
the grid (mesh)
• Procedures to estimate an uncertainty that bounds the
numerical effects is derived directly from the GCI

Simulation input uncertainty
(uinput)
• Both Mean Value (MV, propagation of variance)
and Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) approaches
are described for propagating simulation input
uncertainty through the simulation software
• Provides an estimate of uinput that characterizes a
range of the simulation that can be attributed to
input uncertainty

Experimental uncertainty (uD )

• Both Mean Value (MV) and Latin Hypercube
Sampling (LHS) are discussed in V&V 20

V&V-20 Definitions
• Definitions of Error and Uncertainty*
– (δ) error (of measurement): “result of a measurement minus a true value of the
measurand”, and
– (u) uncertainty (of measurement): “parameter, associated with the result of a
measurement, that characterizes the dispersion of the values that could
reasonably be attributed to the measurand”.

• An error δ is then a quantity that has a particular sign and
magnitude
– Assume that any known errors have been removed
– Remaining error is thus of unknown sign and magnitude

• Uncertainty u is estimated with the idea that ±u
characterizes the range containing the error δ.
– Consider u an estimate of the standard deviation

* International Vocabulary of Basic and General Terms in Metrology, 2nd Edition,
International Organization for Standardization, Geneva, Switzerland, 1993.

Summary
• V&V-20 provides a methodology to estimate the
contribution for sources of uncertainty related to
comparing a measurement and simulation model
– Numerical solution uncertainty
– Simulation input parameter uncertainty
– Experimental measurement uncertainty

• An approach for aggregating the uncertainties
and measuring the measurement-simulation
difference relative to the total uncertainty is
provided
• Guidance on interpreting of the comparison is
given and a procedure to characterize model form
error – due to approximations and assumptions in
the simulation – is discussed

4. Design, Execution, and
Analysis
For a complex application validation experiments conducted at
various levels of physics and geometric fidelity
• Constitutive model validation (material characterization) (10 – 100
of exps)
– Representative of intended application environment and materials

• Ensemble Validation (single physics/simple coupling and stylized
hardware) (10s of experiments)
– Study accuracy over a range of the conditions
– May do this for several of important single physics

• Subsystem Validation (Dominant physical coupling and
representative but simplified hardware) (> 10s of exps)
• Full system Validation (full coupling with actual or slightly simplified
hardware) (1 exp)
• Designing the suite of experiments is a challenging activity
• “Top-Down” or “Bottom-up” are typical hierarchal validation approaches
24

6. Adequacy Assessment
• Is the measured accuracy (value of the metric)
adequate for the intended application??
• Judging model adequacy at lower levels in the
validation hierarchy can be difficult
– Measured quantities at lower levels may be related, but not
directly applicable to higher levels and the intended application
– Lower level validation may be a more highly stressing
application of the model than at higher levels

• One approach is based on measuring the
comparison relative to uncertainty
– Judge if the simulation model has adequate uncertainty relative
to the exp data -> if so, the simulation model including all
uncertainties is adequate for application at higher levels and for
an intended application
25

Intended Application
• Goal of validation is to validate the simulation model for
uses other than where validation is performed (an intended
application)
• The intended application needs to be carefully considered.
Questions to consider:
– What is the application of interest to apply the simulation model?
– What simulation outputs are needed and for what simulation
environments?
» Lift/drag, maximum temperature, temperature difference, incident
heat flux, etc.
» Subsonic/supersonic, shock loading, Fire-level fluxes
– What is the required accuracy?
» 20%, factor of 2, conservative

• Clear line-of-sight back to the intended application is
needed for a validation activity to be successful
– Success doesn’t necessarily require the model to be acceptable

How do we move beyond the
(validation) process level??
• ASME V&V-20 committee developed a standard for
“Verification and Validation in Computational Fluid Dynamics
and Heat Transfer” (due late summer/early fall)
• Standard is based on definitions of error and uncertainty
that have been internationally codified by the experimental
community over several decades
• Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement
(GUM) was published by the International Organization for
Standardization (ISO) in its name and those of six other
international organizations.
– Bureau International des Poids et Mesures (BIPM), International Electrotechnical
Commission (IEC), International Federation of Clinical Chemistry (IFCC), International
Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC), International Union of Pure and Applied
Physics (IUPAP), and International Organization of Legal Metrology (OIML)

Validation methodology developed
using error and uncertainty concepts
• These (error and uncertainty) concepts were first extended
in [Coleman and Stern, JFE 1997] for Validation
• V&V-20 Validation Methodology
– Scope was to quantify the degree of accuracy inferred from the
comparison of solution (simulation) and data for a specified variable at
a specified validation point
– Approaches for estimating the uncertainty in the experimental
response (based on ASME PTC 19.1)
– Approaches for code and solution verification (Method of Manufactured
Solutions, MMS, and Grid Convergence Index, GCI)
– Approaches for estimating uncertainty due to uncertainty model inputs
(MV and LHS)
– Metric for comparing simulation and data while including all previously
mentioned uncertainties
