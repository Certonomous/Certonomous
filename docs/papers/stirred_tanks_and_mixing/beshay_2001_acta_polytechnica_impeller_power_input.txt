WHY THIS PAPER WAS RETRIEVED, AND WHAT IT SETTLES
It was fetched to answer one question the cfd-supervisor put in writing: is the
power-number deficit of our MRF Rushton case explained, QUANTITATIVELY, by blade
thickness, from a source the box actually holds? Before this retrieval the box
held NO such source -- only Reid et al. 2025, which says nothing about blade
thickness and reports no measured power number at all.

This paper supplies two things, and they are of different evidentiary weight:

  1. MEASURED, and at our exact tank ratios. Its SMALL test rig (Table 1,
     Table 3, section 2) is T = 0.300 m, H = T, four wall baffles of width
     b = 0.1 T, impeller D = 100 mm, D/T = 1/3, l/D = 0.25, w/D = 0.2,
     D1/D = 0.75, six blades, water at 20 C, strain-gauge torquemeter,
     3e4 < Re < 6e4. EVERY ONE OF THOSE RATIOS IS OURS, and our Re = 5.0e4 is
     inside their range. Its measured standard-Rushton power number is
     Po = 5.41 at h/T = 0.33 (our clearance) and 5.44 at h/T = 0.5. Its impeller
     thickness is t = 1.55 mm on D = 100 mm, i.e. t/D = 0.0155; OURS IS
     t = 4.00 mm, t/D = 0.0400. Stated scatter: average relative standard
     deviation in the range 2.3 to 16 %.
  2. SECONDARY, a correlation it reproduces rather than derives. Its eq. (5)
     gives the Bujalski, Nienow, Chatwin & Cooke (1987) correlation
         Po = 2.512 (t/D)^-0.195 (T/T0)^0.063 ,  T0 = 1 m
     attributed to Chem. Eng. Sci. 42(2), 317-326. BUJALSKI 1987 ITSELF IS NOT
     ON THIS BOX. The correlation is therefore a SECONDARY-SOURCE reproduction,
     and the source we hold DOES NOT STATE ITS RANGE OF VALIDITY IN t/D. Our
     t/D = 0.0400 may lie outside the data it was fitted to; a reader must treat
     any evaluation at that thickness as an EXTRAPOLATION OF UNKNOWN REACH.

  Cross-check the paper itself reports for the correlation: Po = 6.2 predicted
  against 6.4 measured on the large rig, and 5.3 predicted against 5.425
  measured on the small rig -- so roughly 2 to 3 % on its own rigs.

Acta Polytechnica Vol. 41 No. 6/2001




Power Input of High-Speed Rotary
Impellers
K. R. Beshay, J. Kratěna, I. Fořt, O. Brůha
This paper presents the results of an experimental investigation of the power input of pitched blade impellers and standard Rushton turbine
impellers in a cylindrical vessel provided with four radial baffles at its wall under a turbulent regime of flow of an agitated liquid. The
influence of the geometry of the pitched blade impellers (pitch angle, number of blades) and the off-bottom impeller clearance of both
high-speed impellers tested on the impeller power input is determined in two sizes of the cylindrical vessel (0.3 m and 0.8 m diameter of
vessel). A strain gauge torquemeter is used in the small vessel and a phase shift mechanical torquemeter is used in the large vessel. All results
of the experiments correspond to the condition that the Reynolds number modified for the impeller exceeds ten thousand. The results of this
study show that the significant influence of the separating disk thickness of the turbine impeller corresponds fairly well to the empirical
equations presented in the literature. Both the influence of the number of impeller blades and the blade pitch angle of the pitched blade
impeller were expressed quantitatively by means of the power dependence of the recently published correlations: the higher the pitch angle
and the number of blades, the higher the values of the impeller power input. Finally, it follows from results of this study that the impeller
off-bottom clearance has a weak influence on the power input of the Rushton turbine impeller, but with decreasing impeller off-bottom
clearance the power input of the pitched blade impeller increases significantly.

Keywords: impeller power input, Rushton turbine, pitched blade impeller.
