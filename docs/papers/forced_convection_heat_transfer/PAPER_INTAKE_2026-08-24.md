## 3. Two-reader assessment — Bahrami 2005 (NASA/TM–2005-212841)

**What this paper is:** a **computational** study. Bahrami models a flat plate
with an unheated starting section and a step wall-temperature distribution using
the GASP RANS code with three closures (Baldwin–Lomax, k-ω, SST), and compares
against experiment. **The experimental data are Moretti & Kays (1965), ref. 2 —
not Bahrami's own measurements, and that paper is not on disk.** Bahrami is
therefore a **secondary** for any experimental quantity.

### (a) What it can arm

- **Rungs:** the **T1 / T1b / T1c** forced-convection canon (flat plate). It is
  the only flat-plate *thermal* forced-convection document in this library.
- **Measured quantity:** **Stanton number `St` against `Re_x`**, for both a
  uniform wall temperature and a step wall temperature, with an insulated
  starting length.
  - `St = q / (c_p∞ ρ∞ U∞ ΔT)`, `Re = ρ∞ U∞ x / µ∞` (both printed definitions).
  - **Conditions as printed:** `U∞ = 19.39 m/s`, `T∞ = 309.4 K`,
    `ρ∞ = 1.185 kg/m³`, `Pr = 0.72`, `Pr_t = 0.9`; insulated starting length
    leading edge to `x₀ = 0.61 m`; overall plate length `l = 1.83 m`; flow
    tripped and fully turbulent, no pressure gradient.
  - **Figure 3** — uniform wall temperature against the von Kármán analogy.
    **Figure 4** — step wall temperature against the experiments.
    **Figure 5** — step and uniform, plates shifted.
- **Uncertainty AS PRINTED IN THE PAPER:** *"The overall uncertainties in
  measurement of temperature, heat flux, and velocity in the cooled section were
  estimated to be approximately 3%, 2%, and 1%, respectively"* (p. 4), repeated
  later and there **attributed to Moretti and Kays (ref. 2)**. Also printed:
  the cooled section was *"somewhat non-uniform in temperature, with the mean
  variations of order 2 K"*.

### (b) What it cannot arm

- **It is not a primary.** Every experimental symbol belongs to Moretti & Kays
  (1965), which is **not on disk**. A gate row citing "the experiment" through
  Bahrami cites a paper this lab has not seen.
- **No combined uncertainty band on `St` is printed anywhere.** Three *component*
  uncertainties are given (3 % / 2 % / 1 %); the paper never combines them into a
  band on the plotted quantity. Combining them would be **the lab's assumption,
  not the paper's statement**, and §4(c) explains why that matters.
- **The boundary condition is not a clean step.** The paper states explicitly
  that the real experiment departed from the ideal: the starting section was
  *"somewhat non-uniform"*, the cooled section non-uniform by ~2 K, and *"nature
  provided a continuously varying temperature profile in the region between the
  cooled and uncooled sections, rather than a sharp discontinuity."* Bahrami
  handled this by entering the **point-wise measured plate temperature profile**
  into GASP. **That point-wise profile is not tabulated in this document** — only
  plotted (Figure 2). A reproduction that imposes an idealised step is
  reproducing a different problem from the one the figures grade.
- **No inlet turbulence quantities** are given (no inlet `k`, no length scale, no
  free-stream turbulence intensity).
- **Model-side numbers are GASP results**, not measurements, and must never be
  read across as data.

### (c) Digitisability

**Figure-digitisation only. There are no data tables in this document** — the
only occurrence of the word "Table" is "Table of Contents".

| Property | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Axes | **Linear both.** `St × 10E3` on the ordinate, `Re × 10E–6` on the abscissa |
| Ordinate range / ticks | 1.0 to 4.5, labelled every 0.5 |
| Abscissa range / ticks | 0.5 to 2.5, labelled every 0.5 |
| Series per figure | up to five (Experiment Moretti; Baldwin–Lomax shifted; k-ω shifted; SST shifted; von Kármán shifted) |

**Achievable tolerance, and how it was judged.** Both axes are linear with
labelled decade-free ticks every 0.5 unit, so the scale is set by two well
separated fiducials rather than by interpolating a log decade — that is the
easy case. Judging from tick spacing against marker size, an isolated
experimental symbol is locatable to roughly **±0.05 in `St × 10³`**, i.e.
**≈ ±2 % at `St × 10³ ≈ 2.5`**. Series separation is not the limiting factor:
the paper's own text puts the models 10–15 % from the experiment, which is
several times the marker-centroid error, so the curves are distinguishable
where they do not cross.

**Where an honest band cannot be stated.** The **±2 % is a digitisation
tolerance, not an experimental uncertainty**, and the two must not be added and
reported as one number. Because the paper prints no combined band on `St`, a
gate row wanting "experiment ± band" on Stanton number **cannot take that band
from this paper.** It could take 3 %/2 %/1 % on temperature, heat flux and
velocity — second-hand, attributed to a paper not on disk — and would then have
to propagate them itself. **That propagation is the lab's construction and must
be labelled as such**, per the standing prohibition on presenting an estimate as
a measurement.

## 4. Two-reader assessment — Narumanchi, Hassani & Bharathan 2005 (NREL/TP-540-38787)

**What this paper is:** a **CFD modelling report** (FLUENT) on single-phase and
nucleate-boiling liquid jet impingement cooling of an IGBT power-electronics
package. Its "experimental" comparison column is **evaluations of other authors'
correlations**, not measurements.

### (a) What it can arm

Honestly: **very little as a gate primary**, and its relevance is to the
**F14 DC-cooling spine** rather than to any T-family rung — it is a
liquid-cooling document, and the F14 spine is air-cooled.

What it does carry, concretely:

- **Table 3.1** *(printed p. 17)* — `h_avg` from correlations vs `h_avg` from
  FLUENT, with the percentage difference, across three configurations:
  - **Single circular submerged jet** (Womac et al. [5]): `d = 3.1 mm`,
    `D = 14.3 mm`, `S_NP = 4d`; `Re_d = 9,300` → 27,300 vs 26,400 W/m²K (3 %);
    `Re_d = 46,400` → 69,300 vs 81,400 W/m²K (16 %).
  - **Single circular free-surface jet** (Womac et al. [5]): `Re_d = 3,100` →
    11,500 vs 14,000 (20 %); `Re_d = 9,300` → 19,600 vs 22,500 (14 %);
    `Re_d = 46,400` → 45,700 vs 61,000 (29 %).
  - **Single circular submerged and confined jet** (Garimella & Rice [11]):
    `d = 3.2 mm`, `D = 11.3 mm`, `S_NP = 4d`; `Re_d = 4,100` → 18,300 vs 19,200
    (5 %); `Re_d = 10,300` → 34,800 vs 34,800 (0 %); `Re_d = 22,100` → 59,100
    vs 54,500 (8 %).
- A **mesh-independence statement**: *"all the CFD results presented in this
  section are independent of the spatial mesh to within 5%"* — a claim, with no
  grid triple and no order of accuracy behind it.
- Material property tables (2.1, 4.1, 4.2, 9.1) and Appendix A fluid properties
  from Aspen, which are usable as **property references**.

### (b) What it cannot arm

- **The comparison column is correlations, not data.** The Table 3.1 header
  reads literally `h_avg from correlations (W/m2K)`. **There is not a single
  measured point in it.** A correlation evaluated at a condition is a model
  output; grading a solver against it grades two models against each other.
- **No uncertainty is attached to any value in Table 3.1** — not to the
  correlation column, not to the CFD column. This is the **correlation-only,
  no-band** case named as a disqualifier: a gate row needing an experimental
  band cannot get one here.
- **The 5 % mesh-independence claim is not a grid-convergence study.** No triple,
  no observed order, no GCI. Under standing rule 5 it could not be graded as one.
- **Wrong working fluid and wrong regime for the F14 air-cooled spine** — water,
  glycol-water and R-113 jets onto a chip stack, some with nucleate boiling.
- The boiling-side validations (Katto & Kunihiro [33], Zhou & Ma [28]) are
  against **third-party experiments not on disk**.

### (c) Digitisability

**Best case on form, worst case on content — and the two must not be confused.**

- **Table 3.1 is tabulated**, so its numbers need **no digitisation at all**:
  transcription tolerance is **exact**, zero error. Same for Tables 2.1, 4.1,
  4.2, 9.1, 9.2 and Appendix A.
- **But a tolerance of zero on a number that has no uncertainty band, and which
  is a correlation evaluation rather than a measurement, buys nothing for a
  gate.** Exactness of transcription is not evidential quality. Stating "±0 %"
  for these values would be true and misleading in the same breath.
- Remaining results are figures with no digitisation advantage over the above.

**Plain statement: a claimed experimental band cannot be honestly stated for
anything in this report, because none is printed and the quantities are not
measurements.**

## 5. Meinders 1998 — the coverage-cell assessment

This is the paper that matters. It is a **3D forced-convection experimental
primary with local, resolved, conjugate heat-transfer measurements**, and it is
already HELD as T5's primary.

### 5.1 The directive claim, tested

`docs/campaigns/T-family/THERMAL_BUILDUP_DIRECTIVE.md` line 39 (H-1(b)) records:

> Open, title-page verified, stated uncertainty 5 % mid-face / 10 % edges in local `h`

**VERDICT: the claim HOLDS.** It is printed, verbatim, at **printed page 59**
(PDF page 61), in §3.2.4 under the subsection heading **"Overall accuracy of the
heat transfer coefficient"**:

> "From the above analysis of the different contributions to the experimental
> uncertainty it is concluded that the accumulation of errors results in
> approximately 5% accuracy in the local heat transfer coefficient for the
> mid-region of the five faces of the cube. The heat transfer coefficients at
> the edges have a somewhat higher uncertainty of about 10 % due to, among
> others, mapping inaccuracies."

Two points of precision the directive's shorthand compresses, recorded so no
later reader over-reads it:

1. The paper says **"the mid-region of the five faces"**, not "mid-face". Five
   faces, because the sixth is the mounting interface. The directive's "mid-face"
   is a fair contraction.
2. The 5 % / 10 % applies to the **local** heat transfer coefficient `h`, which
   is what the directive says. **It is not a Nusselt-number uncertainty**, and
   the thesis does not report its own results as Nusselt numbers at all — see
   §5.5.

A separate 10 % figure appears at printed p. 230 for **reproducibility across
cubes** (*"the results were well within the 10% uncertainty limit"*). It is a
different statement from the edge uncertainty and should not be conflated with it.

**The supporting uncertainty budget, as printed** (§3.2.4, printed pp. 54–59):

| Contribution | Printed value |
| --- | --- |
| Epoxy thermal conductivity `λ` | 0.24 W/mK, accuracy **2 %** (two independent methods, Appendix A) |
| Epoxy layer thickness `d` | 1.5 mm, uncertainty **0.01 mm**, machined |
| Black paint emissivity `ε` | 0.95, experimental uncertainty **2 %**; paint layer < 0.06 mm |
| Thermocouples (type T, copper-constantan) | **0.1 °C** after calibration; accuracy estimated **within 0.5 °C** with radiative exchange |
| Copper core temperature | **0.1 °C**, embedded calibrated thermocouple |
| IR surface temperature, **mid-plane** | absolute accuracy **0.4 °C** |
| IR surface temperature, **near edges** | about **0.6 °C**, from image-mapping effects |
| Truncation error, 30 × 30 grid (3 cells through thickness) | **within 3 %** |
| Truncation error, local `h`: mid-plane / edges | order **2 %** / **5 %** |
| Base-plate + lead-wire heat leakage through the sixth face | **10–15 %** of supplied power, smaller at higher `Re` |

The uncertainty expression itself is printed as Eq. (3.19), a full single-sample
propagation in `δλ, δT_co, δT_amb, δT_ad, δd, δε, δT_sur`. **The 5 %/10 % is the
result of a stated propagation, not an assertion** — which is what makes it
usable.

### 5.2 Geometry and array spacing, as printed

- **Cube:** `H = 15 mm`, wall-mounted on the channel floor.
- **Channel:** rectangular, height `D = 51 mm`, width **600 mm**;
  width-to-height ratio **12**, which the paper says ensures two-dimensional flow
  at the centre of the test section. **`D/H = 3.4`.**
- **Tunnel:** closed, blowing-type, low-Reynolds; flow recirculated by two fans;
  two heat exchangers thermostat the air; honeycombs and fine grids upstream;
  contraction ~2 m upstream of the test section.
- **Test section oriented vertically**, so **gravity acts in the spanwise (`z`)
  direction** — not aligned with either the streamwise or the wall-normal axis.
- **Four configurations studied:**
  - single cube;
  - in-line row of cubes, `S_x/H = 2`;
  - tandem of two cubes, various `S_x/H` and `S_z/H`;
  - **regular matrix, `S_x/H = S_z/H = 4`.**
- **The matrix in detail** (printed p. 211): **25 × 10 cubes**, streamwise ×
  spanwise; streamwise and spanwise **centre-to-centre distance 45 mm**, giving
  `S/H = 4`; boundary layer tripped ~1 m upstream of the first column; **at
  least 15 rows were needed to make the flow fully periodic**, and measurements
  were taken **around the 18th row**, at mid-height.

### 5.4 The thermal boundary condition — conjugate, and fully specified

**It is conjugate, by construction, and the paper is unusually explicit.**

- The measurement cube is a **copper core surrounded by an epoxy layer**
  (Fig. 3.4, printed p. 41).
- **Epoxy layer: 1.5 mm thick ±0.01 mm, `λ = 0.24 W/mK` ±2 %, isotropic.**
- **Copper core held at 75 °C for all matrix heat-transfer measurements**
  (printed p. 230). The copper is uniform to **within 0.3 °C** even at large
  dissipation.
- The cubes are mounted on a **formaldehyde base plate, `λ = 0.33 W/mK`,
  uncertainty about 10 %**, chosen low to suppress conduction loss. Measured
  leakage through the sixth face is nonetheless **10–15 %** of supplied power.
- Surfaces are **black-painted, `ε = 0.95` ±2 %**, layer < 0.06 mm.
- **It is neither isothermal-surface nor uniform-flux.** The *outer surface*
  temperature is a solved field with large spatial gradients — Fig. 8.23 shows
  `T_s` ranging roughly 43–62 °C around a single 15 mm cube at `Re_H = 3854`,
  against a 75 °C core. The method **solves the Laplace equation inside the
  epoxy layer** on a 30 × 30 surface grid (3 cells through the thickness) to get
  the local conductive flux. **The experiment is a conjugate problem and was
  reduced as one.**
- **The reference temperature is the adiabatic one.** For the matrix,
  printed p. 229: *"Heat transfer was measured at one powered cube in the
  spatially periodic matrix flow, while all other cubes remained unpowered. The
  inlet temperature was used as a reference which, in this case, equals the
  adiabatic surface temperature."* **Inlet temperature 19–21 °C**, measured for
  each experiment and used in processing. For the **9-cube in-line array**, by
  contrast, **all cubes were powered** and the adiabatic reference was obtained
  in a **separate** experiment with the cube of interest unpowered.

**Why this matters more than any other line in this record:** `h` here is
`h_ad`, defined against the **adiabatic** reference temperature in the
Moffat–Anderson sense, **not** against a bulk or film temperature. A comparator
that forms `h = q''/(T_s − T_bulk)` is not computing the plotted quantity. In
the **matrix** case the two coincide because only one cube is heated — the paper
says so explicitly — which is precisely what makes the matrix case cheap to
reproduce correctly and the 9-cube array expensive.

### 5.5 Measurement technique, as printed

- **Infrared thermography** for the surface temperature field — the load-bearing
  measurement. Channel roof replaced by an IR-transmitting foil window. In-situ
  calibration plus an image-restoration technique give **0.4 °C** mid-plane,
  **0.6 °C** near edges. Native resolution supports **30 × 30 surface cells per
  face**, artificially doubled to 60 × 60 by interpolation (which the paper is
  careful to say *"does not add information"*).
- **Liquid Crystal Thermography** as an independent surface-temperature check —
  Fig. 8.23 shows the two agreeing **within the claimed 0.4 °C**.
- **Laser Doppler Anemometry** (three components) for the velocity field, on a
  traverse with **0.1 mm** absolute location accuracy and better than 0.1 mm
  reproducibility. §4.3.3–4.3.5 carry a full LDA accuracy analysis.
- **A separate fast-responding heat flux sensor** for the *time-dependent*
  convective heat flux: a 0.2 µm platinum film on a polished glass substrate
  with two vacon pins, active area 4 × 4 mm, ~3.9 Ω, constant-temperature
  operation, sampled at 300–500 Hz. **The paper states its own purpose was
  detecting characteristic frequencies, "rather than determining the absolute
  value of the time-dependent convective heat flux."** That sensor's output is
  therefore **not** a quantitative flux primary, and must not be used as one.
- Supporting: oil-film and smoke visualisation, static pressure, topology-based
  structure identification.

### 5.6 Which coverage cells this could close

The lab's own statement of the gap is `docs/THERMAL_CAPABILITY_STATE.md` §0:
*"There is no forced-convection heat-transfer rung, no mixed-convection rung,
and no conjugate rung"*, and §4: *"The thermal side cannot currently build a
forced-convection rung, because it has nothing to grade one against."*

| Coverage cell | Could Meinders 1998 close it? | The evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forced convection, external** | **YES — and the paper classifies itself.** | Printed p. 232: *"the heat transfer displays a full symmetry indicating that local buoyancy effects are negligible. **The flow is, therefore, classified as forced convection for all Reynolds numbers considered.**"* Printed p. 39 adds that for most experiments `Re` was large enough to neglect buoyancy, and flags explicitly where it is not. This is the structural gap named in `THERMAL_CAPABILITY_STATE.md` §4, and this paper closes the reference side of it. |
| **3D thermal** | **YES, irreducibly.** | A wall-mounted cube in a channel is 3D by construction; local `h` is resolved over **all five exposed faces** on a 30 × 30 grid per face, with paths that wrap the cube (ABCDA, ABCD). There is no 2D reduction of this case. |
| **Conjugate heat transfer** | **YES, and it is the strongest of the three.** | Copper core at 75 °C, 1.5 mm epoxy layer `λ = 0.24 W/mK`, formaldehyde base `λ = 0.33 W/mK`; the outer surface temperature is a *solved conjugate field*, not an imposed BC, and the paper's own data reduction solves the Laplace equation in the solid. A solid region is not optional in a faithful reproduction. |
| Mixed convection | **NO.** | The paper deliberately operates where buoyancy is negligible and says so. It cannot substitute for Blay 1992 on K0d. |

**All three cells are addressed by a single reference, with a stated
propagated uncertainty, in one geometry.** That is unusual and is the reason

### 5.7 Cost class — a recommendation, not a registration

**No cost is registered here and none is asserted as measured** (standing rule
12). What already exists: `docs/campaigns/T-family/T5_PREREGISTRATION_DRAFT.md`
(written 2026-08-22, **unfrozen**, 12 INTERPRETATIONs on Sanaa's desk) registers

**One observation this intake adds, offered to the supervisor as a
recommendation:**

The draft is built on the **single cube at `Re_H` 4440**. The **matrix** case is
the cheaper and evidentially stronger target, for reasons that are printed
facts rather than preferences:

1. **The matrix is spatially periodic and the single cube is not.** Printed
   p. 211: the matrix *"served as reference dataset for the validation of
   numerical simulations at ERCOFTAC workshops"* (TU Delft, 6–7 June 1997;
   UMIST Manchester, 28–29 May 1998), and *"the usefulness of this test case is
   directly related to the **well defined periodic boundary conditions**
   involved which can easily be implemented in numerical simulations."*
   Measurements sit at the 18th row, *"independent of the inflow conditions."*
2. **That removes the single cube's worst modelling problem.** For the single
   cube the oncoming flow is a **developing turbulent boundary layer on the floor
   and a developing *laminar* boundary layer on the opposing wall** — asymmetric —
   with a measured core acceleration of **`dU∞/dx = 0.67 1/s`** (printed p. 123).
   That inlet is not synthesisable; it must be digitised from Fig. 5.2/5.3, and
   the digitisation error then propagates into every graded row.
3. **A periodic domain is one spatial period** — a 45 × 45 mm footprint, 51 mm
   tall, one cube — instead of a channel plus development length. **The cell
   count for equal near-wall resolution is therefore substantially lower**, which
   is a statement about domain volume and not a measured runtime.
4. **Only one cube is heated** in the matrix (printed p. 229), so the thermal
   problem is one conjugate solid region against a periodic hydrodynamic field,
   with the reference temperature equal to the measured inlet temperature.

**Cost class, stated honestly:** the same class as the registered single-cube
ladder — a 3D steady conjugate RANS ladder, single-digit USD at the draft's own
rate models — and **plausibly cheaper per rung**, on the domain-volume argument
above. **That "plausibly cheaper" is an argument from geometry, not a
measurement, and it must be costed in a pre-registration before any compute**,
exactly as rule 12 requires. Nothing here authorises a run.

### 5.8 Digitisability of Meinders

**Figure-digitisation only.** The appendices (A.1 epoxy, A.2 formaldehyde base
plate) are **thermal-conductivity measurements**, not heat-transfer data tables.
**There is no tabulated `h` or `Nu` anywhere in the thesis.** This confirms
`T_FAMILY_INDEX.md:45`'s "data are digitisable figures, no tabulated appendix".

**Figure 8.24 — surface-averaged `h` vs `Re_H` (printed p. 231).** The single
most gradeable figure: dimensional `h` in W/m²K, six Reynolds numbers, six
symbol classes — (+) side north, (o) front, (×) side south, (△) rear, (□) top,
(◇) cube average. Axes **linear both**; ordinate 0 → 120.0 W/m²K labelled at
40.0/80.0/120.0 with minor ticks; abscissa `Re_H` 2000 → 6000 labelled every
1000.

- **Front (o) and rear (△) are cleanly separated** at every `Re` station and are
  digitisable to roughly **±2 W/m²K, i.e. ≈ ±3 % at `h ≈ 60 W/m²K`** — judged
  from the ordinate scale (120 W/m²K across the plot box) against marker
  diameter, which puts centroid location well inside one minor tick.
- **The cube average (◇) is NOT separably digitisable from this figure.** At
  every `Re` station the (+), (×), (□) and (◇) markers are overprinted into a
  single blob around 45–75 W/m²K. **A claimed cube-averaged `h` band read off
  Fig. 8.24 could not be honestly stated**, and this is worth flagging because
  the cube average is the most natural scalar to gate on.
- Fig. 8.25 (supplied power and through-face heat flow vs `Re_H`, 0 → 4.5 W)
  digitises comparably but is an energy-budget diagnostic, not a gate quantity.

**Figure 8.26 — local `h/h̄` along paths ABCDA and ABCD (printed p. 232).**
Ordinate **linear**, 0 → 2.5, labelled every 0.5 with minor ticks; six Reynolds
series.

- **Mid-face plateau values: ≈ ±0.03 in `h/h̄`, about 3 %** — from the ordinate
  scale against marker size, in the flat regions where the six series collapse.
- **Near an edge, no honest band can be stated, and the reason is the
  abscissa.** The abscissa is **"location on path" and is not numeric** — it
  carries only the corner landmarks A, B, C, D. Recovering a streamwise position
  requires *assuming* uniform arc-length parametrisation between corners. In the
  flat mid-face regions that assumption costs almost nothing; **at the corners,
  where the profiles fall almost vertically from ~1.0 to ~0.2 within a few
  pixels, a small abscissa error maps to a very large ordinate error.**
  So the digitisation error follows the same mid-face/edge split as the paper's
  own 5 %/10 %, but for an independent reason, and **the two must be reported as
  separate channels, never summed into one band.**
- The ordinate is **normalised by the cube-averaged `h`**, so recovering
  dimensional local `h` requires `h̄` from Fig. 8.24 — where the (◇) cube-average
  symbol is exactly the one inside the overprint cluster. **This couples the two
  weaknesses: the normalisation constant is the least readable number in the
  figure set.** A rung wanting dimensional local `h` must confront this; a rung
  gating on the *normalised* profile shape need not.

**Figure 8.23 — surface temperature `T_s` along the same paths (printed p. 230).**
Ordinate linear 40 → 70 °C labelled every 10 °C. Digitisable to about
**±0.3 °C**, which is **the same order as the paper's own 0.4 °C measurement
accuracy** — so digitisation neither dominates nor is negligible, and both
channels must be carried. Contains an independent liquid-crystal vs infrared
cross-check.

**Figure 5.2 / 5.3 (single cube inlet profiles)** are digitisable but, per §5.7,
are the single cube's structural weakness rather than a gradeable output.

**Nusselt number: the thesis does not report one.** Its own results are `h` in
W/m²K and `h/h̄`. `Nu` appears only in the Chapter 2 literature review of other
authors' correlations. A rung wanting `Nu = hH/λ_air` must form it, and **the
paper does not fix the temperature at which `λ_air` is evaluated** — with a
75 °C core, ~19–21 °C inlet and a 43–62 °C surface, film-temperature choice is a
real and non-negligible degree of freedom. **That choice is the lab's and must be
pre-registered, not inherited.**
