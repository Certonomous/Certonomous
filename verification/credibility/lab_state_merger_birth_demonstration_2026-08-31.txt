planted control, on a scratch tree. EVERY BYTE IT READS WAS WRITTEN BY THE

SELFTEST, and its mutation controls
------------------------------------
  python3 scripts/merge_lab_state.py --selftest    -> 30/30 cases, rc 0

  The selftest was itself mutation-tested: five clauses of the validator were
  removed one at a time in a scratch copy and the selftest was re-run. Each
  removal broke exactly the cases that clause exists to protect -- so the
  30 green cases are a measured population, not an empty one:

    clause removed                          selftest result
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------
    missing source refused -> skipped       C1, C12, C13 FAIL (26/30)
    '## ' heading-count clause              C6 FAIL          (28/30)
    merged-board-copy clause                C10 FAIL         (28/30)
    heading-only truncation guard           C4 FAIL          (28/30)
  (F1 also fails under mutation because the scratch copy has no
   harness/teams.yaml; that failure is an artefact of the mutation rig,
   not of the clause under test, and is named rather than netted out.)

  One real fail-open was found and fixed inside the selftest during this work:
  the refusal-checking helper returned `(why in str(e)) or True`, which would
  have passed EVERY refusal case on ANY error, including the wrong error. With
  the fix in place, removing the merged-board-copy clause makes C10 fail even
  though the build still raised -- because the refusal raised was the wrong
  one. That is the clause's own control, and it was earned by a defect.
