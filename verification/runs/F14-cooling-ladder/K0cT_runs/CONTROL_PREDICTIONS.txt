C2 -- RAYLEIGH NUMBER RAISED 30 PERCENT.  KIND: RECOGNITION AT THE GATE'S OWN SCALE.
Case C2_hi_c_Ra130: exact twin of T_hi_c with dT (hence Ra) multiplied by 1.30.

THE PREDICTION IS DERIVED FROM THE REFERENCE'S OWN TWO-Ra PAIR, BEFORE THE RUN,
and from nothing this rung produced.  Spec Section 2.3 gives the mid-height peak
mean velocities at both Rayleigh numbers:

    up-peak    +0.140 m/s at Ra 0.86e6   ->  +0.190 m/s at Ra 1.43e6
    down-peak  -0.135 m/s at Ra 0.86e6   ->  -0.189 m/s at Ra 1.43e6

so V_peak ~ Ra^n with
    n_up = ln(0.190/0.140) / ln(1.43/0.86) = 0.6004
    n_dn = ln(0.189/0.135) / ln(1.43/0.86) = 0.6615

A 30 percent rise in Ra therefore gives 1.30^n:

    PREDICTED shift in peak |V| against this case's own coarse twin:
        +17.4 percent to +19.4 percent
    which EXCEEDS the gate's 15 percent band on the velocity rows.
    gate verdict on the velocity rows for this case: FAIL

THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING CONTROL.  A 15 percent band that could not see a 30
percent error in the driving parameter would make the rung's pass mean nothing.
IN-LOG WITNESS OF THE PLANT: the hotT and coldT function objects make the
RUNNING SOLVER print its own wall temperatures every 50 iterations.  Their
difference must read 51.87 K against the graded case's 39.90 K, a ratio of
exactly 1.300.  A plant living only in 0.orig/T would not move those lines.

C4 -- TURBULENCE SEED RAISED 100x.  KIND: RECOGNITION OF A CONFOUND.
Case S_hi_c_seed100: exact twin of T_hi_c with the initial k multiplied by 100
(and omega seeded consistently from the same mixing length).

A RANS solve of a weakly turbulent buoyant cavity can be seed-determined: too
low a seed relaminarises, too high a seed leaves an eddy viscosity that never
decays.  Either way the "answer" would be an initial condition.

PREDICTION: the converged answer does not move by a tenth of its own band.
    |S_seed100 - S_baseline|            <= 0.005      (a tenth of the 0.05 band)
    |V_peak_seed100/V_peak_baseline - 1| <= 1.5 percent (a tenth of the 15 percent band)
If either moves more, the answer is seed-determined and the graded rows are
REFUSED, not graded.

===============================================================================
THE ATTRIBUTION RULE, REGISTERED BEFORE ANY RESULT IS READ
===============================================================================
A deviation from the reference could be the mesh, the model, the boundary
conditions, or the reference's own uncertainty.  A rung that cannot tell them
apart has not validated anything.  So, for each graded quantity q:

    D_mesh  = |q(fine)          - q(coarse)|          same case
    D_model = |q(kOmegaSST)     - q(LaunderSharmaKE)| fine mesh, hi Ra
    D_bc    = |q(measured ends) - q(adiabatic ends)|  coarse mesh, hi Ra
    U_ref   = the reference's OWN increment: 0.02 on S (its fit residual), and
              3.6 percent on the velocity peaks (its measured antisymmetry defect)
    E       = |q(solve) - q(reference)|

  1. E <= U_ref                      -> agreement.  Nothing to attribute.
  2. E > U_ref, and one of D_mesh, D_model, D_bc is within a factor of 2 of E
                                     -> that one is the prime suspect, named.
  3. E > U_ref and ALL of D_mesh, D_model, D_bc are below E/2
                                     -> none of the three explains it.  The
                                        deviation is charged to what is COMMON
                                        to every case: the model CLASS, or the
                                        case setup.

AND ONE DIRECTIONAL PREDICTION, WHICH IS FALSIFIABLE AND IS REGISTERED NOW.
OpenFOAM's INCOMPRESSIBLE RAS models carry NO buoyancy production or destruction
term in the k equation.  (`buoyantKEpsilon` exists only in the COMPRESSIBLE
family -- src/TurbulenceModels/compressible/RAS/buoyantKEpsilon -- and cannot be
selected by buoyantBoussinesqSimpleFoam, which constructs an
incompressible::turbulenceModel.  Verified by reading the installed source, not
recalled.)  In a STABLY stratified core that term is a SINK: G_b = -beta g.grad(T)
nut/Prt < 0.  Omitting a sink over-predicts core turbulence, over-mixes the core,
and therefore biases the core stratification S LOW.

    IF S comes out BELOW 0.095 in BOTH models by a similar amount, the missing
    buoyancy sink is the prime suspect and the model twin CANNOT separate it,
    because both models are missing the same term.
    IF S comes out ABOVE 0.095, the missing sink is EXONERATED and the cause is
    elsewhere.

===============================================================================
CONVERGENCE, REGISTERED BEFORE THE RUN
===============================================================================
Standing criterion, docs/physics_rules.yaml thermal block: PEAK-TO-PEAK SPREAD
of the graded quantity over a FIXED window of 400 outer iterations sampled every
50 (9 samples, the block's own minimum).  Not residuals.  Not an endpoint
difference.  Not a fraction of the run.

Applied here to THREE quantities, ALL of which must pass:
  (a) Nu_avg on the hot wall  -- p-p spread < 0.02 percent  (the standing number)
  (b) core stratification S   -- p-p spread < 0.001 absolute (the S band 0.05
      divided by 50, which is the same 1/50 rule the standing number itself was
      derived by, applied to the quantity this gate actually grades)
  (c) peak |Uy| in the domain -- p-p spread < 0.02 percent

A case failing any of the three is REFUSED and not graded.  The criterion is not
loosened and no averaging scheme is invented.  If a graded case cannot be
converged, that is reported as the result.
